## Asheville DSA's pledge on surveillance, October 2026

On Oct 8, 2026, Asheville Democratic Socialists of America (Asheville DSA), a local political organization, posted a pledge it had sent to candidates for Asheville mayor and City Council, Buncombe County Commission, Sheriff, and District Attorney, with responses requested by Oct 12 ([Asheville DSA's page](https://avldsa.org/mass-surveillance/)). The form is titled "North Carolina Candidate Pledge: Ending Mass Surveillance"; the page calls it a pledge on "Ending Unlawful Mass Surveillance." It lists eight commitments, among them: a judicial warrant before any surveillance of a private individual, with a narrow emergency exception; no new surveillance technology or vendor contract without public notice, a 60-day comment period and a recorded vote; ending current vendor contracts within a year of taking office and deleting data not collected under a warrant; no new or extended contracts with Flock Safety, Axon (Fusus), or comparable vendors; and no sharing of surveillance data with an outside agency, including ICE and CBP, without a judicial warrant.

Six candidates' responses are posted on the page:

- **[[Kim Roney]]**, mayor: signed ([signed pledge](https://avldsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Kim-Roney-for-Mayor_NC-Candidate-Pledge-Unlawful-Surveillance.pdf)). Her statement: "Protecting resident data and Constitutional rights from for-profit mass surveillance tech is non-partisan."
- **[[Keith Young]]**, City Council: signed ([signed pledge](https://avldsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/keithyoungconverted.pdf)). His statement: "Changing vendors does not, by itself, resolve concerns about constitutional rights or personal privacy."
- **[[Drew Ball]]**, City Council: did not sign. "While I share the pledge's underlying goals, I have reservations about some of its specific commitments." His objection is to a prohibition based on the vendor rather than the technology: "a blanket prohibition on contracts with vendors such as Axon would also encompass police body cameras."
- **[[Jeffrey Burroughs]]**, City Council: did not sign, and posted a statement of values instead. It proposes adding to the city's quarterly plate reader audit reports, among other items, the reasons for searches and a list of every entity the agency has shared plate reader data with, and commits him to "push to reinstate the Citizens / Police Advisory Committee (CPAC) or another civilian oversight committee."
- **Anna V. Stearns**, County Commission, District 1: "I cannot sign the pledge as written, but I share its underlying commitment to protecting civil liberties." She wrote that commissioners "do not direct the sheriff's law enforcement operations" and that she is reluctant to promise to cancel contracts she has not read.
- **[[Martin Moore]]**, District Attorney: "I am declining to sign the Asheville DSA pledge." His reason: "it is detrimental to the integrity of the role to make a pre-determined choice not to consider types of evidence that our courts deem lawful." He also wrote that his goal in seconding the Oct 6 removal of the sheriff's FUSUS/Axon request was "to receive more information about the request and confirm safeguards are in place."

The page also shows the candidates who did not respond. Each statement is posted in full on [Asheville DSA's page](https://avldsa.org/mass-surveillance/); the lines above are quoted from it. Last verified Oct 14, 2026.

## Mayor

**[[Kim Roney]]**. The record: sole no vote on the Fusus interlocal (Jan 2023), the first RTIC grant ([[2025-03-25 City Council|Mar 2025]]), and the RTIC/Axon package (May 2026). Submitted 29 written questions to APD in March 2025; no public answers had been posted as of Sept 8, 2026. Consulted the ACLU-NC and EFF. On Aug 25, 2026 she moved the Flock termination resolution, which passed 5 to 2, and then moved a moratorium on new plate reader purchases during the city's review, which failed for lack of a second ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]). At the May 28 debate: concerns about "a billionaire-owned, AI-fueled, for-profit tech company... working with ICE and Homeland Security and also the police department to surveil people without a warrant." On the May 12 process: "21 people were sent home without the opportunity to speak." At the Sept 18 Radio Asheville forum: "I've consistently voted against and opposed automated license plate readers... and I made the successful motion to end Asheville's [Flock] contract," and on the city's technology page, "You can look at the Axon agreements because Axon also provides license plate reader technology in our community" ([forum video, 23:07 and 24:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=74GxaJ2LK1Q&t=1387s); [[Kim Roney|more on her page]]). On Oct 8, 2026 she signed Asheville DSA's surveillance pledge ([above](#asheville-dsas-pledge-on-surveillance-october-2026)). ([BPR debate coverage](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance) · [campaign site](https://kimroney4asheville.com/))

**[[Esther Manheimer]]** (incumbent, seeking a 4th term). The record: voted for the Fusus interlocal (Jan 24, 2023), the first RTIC grant (Mar 25, 2025), and the RTIC grant and Axon contract (May 12, 2026), and presided over each meeting. At the May 28, 2026 debate she framed the $1.14M as fiscal efficiency, since the city already pays the Sheriff's Office for Fusus access and the grant lets Asheville "pay for our own," and answered the transparency criticism: "That's not a transparency issue. That's a functioning issue." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance)) On Aug 19, 2026 she called for terminating the Flock contract and removing the cameras: "I have heard clearly from Asheville residents" ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-mayor-esther-manheimer-vote-end-city-flock-safety-contract-remove-cameras-backlash-automatic-license-plate-readers-data-surveillance)). On Aug 25 she voted for the termination, which passed 5 to 2, and confirmed from the dais that the Axon contract covers stationary plate readers: "we did, that is one of the things that we voted for. But we haven't put them up" ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]). At the Sept 18 Radio Asheville forum: "The mayors I talked to across this nation are working really hard to figure out how to get out of these Flock contracts because Flock has grown so large and their platform is so big... And that's why I wrote a resolution to terminate the Flock contract" ([forum video, 21:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=74GxaJ2LK1Q&t=1275s); [[Esther Manheimer|more on her page]]). What remains pending after the vote is on [[Getting Flock Out]]. ([campaign site](https://manheimerformayor.com/))

## Each council candidate, in their words

Quoted in full, with dates and times; forum times are on the audience recording this site holds.

### Maggie Ullman

First public position **Aug 20, 2026**, at the [[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing|agenda briefing]]: residents are saying "get rid of all surveillance... specifically, get rid of Axon," but "the call for 'get rid of Axon'... starts to hit a wall where I can't fathom pulling back on our transparency tools." On Aug 25, 2026 she voted for the Flock termination: "I will support getting rid of these Flocks. I'm glad to hear the manager lay it straight that we're going to have a conversation before any other action would happen. This isn't a bait and switch." ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]) Sept 16 forum: "I'm okay pausing or stopping this and pulling out from the city's 11" (0:40); "unchecked surveillance that is networked nationally to companies we don't know is very concerning" (1:30).

### Antanette Mosley

Aug 25, 2026, on the termination: "My general concern is that what we're attempting to do quite frankly is a bait and switch, and I think we're not giving our citizens credit for seeing that." ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]) Sept 16 forum: Housing Authority residents "requested that we maintain the cameras" (2:10); "I happen to not believe that we have to choose between public safety and civil liberties" (3:09).

### Sheneika Smith

Aug 25, 2026: "I haven't heard any resolutions. I haven't heard about replacements. What do we do?" ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]])

### Drew Ball

First position Aug 14, 2026, in a Mountain Xpress interview published Sept 17: "I didn't think the process of that Council meeting really did anybody justice. It didn't serve the community well, being unrolled like that," with a citizen oversight committee as the next step, in the paper's words ([Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/politics-elections/on-the-record-asheville-city-council-candidate-drew-ball-on-honky-tonk-the-democratic-party-and-government-effectiveness)). At a West Asheville forum on Sept 16, 2026: audit logs, "control our data so it's not fed to a national network," "closed loop systems that we control" (10:09); credited Flock in the paintball case (9:25); "I don't trust the Trump administration or ICE" (10:22) (audience recording held by this site). On Oct 6, 2026, as a county commissioner, he moved to remove the sheriff's $290,000 FUSUS/Axon forfeiture request from the commission's agenda; he stated no reason ([[WNC/Buncombe County/index#The Oct 6, 2026 forfeiture request|Buncombe County]]). In October he declined to sign Asheville DSA's surveillance pledge: "While I share the pledge's underlying goals, I have reservations about some of its specific commitments" ([above](#asheville-dsas-pledge-on-surveillance-october-2026)).

### Keith Young

Closest thing to a stance: his [DSA questionnaire](https://avldsa.org/2026-city-council-primary-questionnaire-responses/) calls for a "local firewall" so city government isn't "feeding a pipeline into enforcement through data, technology, or informal collaboration," plus "strong privacy and data minimization rules" and warrant requirements. At the Sept 16, 2026 forum: "Can we use this system to make our public safety system stronger? Yes, but should it be the one that they agreed upon? Probably not. I think we should have a community conversation" (7:51); did not name the RTIC, Axon, or Flock (audience recording held by this site). In October he signed Asheville DSA's surveillance pledge, with a written statement: "Whether we're discussing Flock, Axon, or Asheville's Real Time Intelligence Center, residents deserve clear answers about what information is collected, who can access it, how long it is retained, and whether it is shared with outside agencies" ([above](#asheville-dsas-pledge-on-surveillance-october-2026)).

## Sheriff: the county camera network is on the ballot

**[[Quentin Miller]]** (D, incumbent, seeking a third term). Built the county's Fusus real-time intelligence center: ~1,800–2,000 cameras including ~1,500 in county schools. Publicly credits it for crime reductions. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/crime-takes-a-dive-in-buncombe-county-sheriff-credits-community-connection-innovative-technology-north-carolina-state-bureau-of-investigations-annual-crime-report-real-time-intelligence-center)) Also on the record: Miller has also taken documented stands *limiting* federal reach: questioning ICE detainers on constitutional grounds and barring ICE from county schools without court orders. ([Bolts](https://boltsmag.org/buncombe-county-district-attorney-sheriff-elections-and-state-preemption/)) The county network has no oversight body outside the sheriff's office ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]).

**[[Gary Parris]]** (R, Navy veteran, former BCSO). In WLOS's Feb 24, 2026 primary profile he pledged a "Unified Operations Intelligence Center to share actionable intelligence with all agencies" and said "As the Sheriff, I don't enforce on behalf of ICE" ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/meet-two-gop-republican-candidates-primary-election-running-buncombe-county-sheriff-gary-parris-victor-vic-morman-vote-voters-immigration-crime-prevention-hiring)). The county already runs an intelligence center on Fusus; the statement does not say whether his would be that one. At the Oct 2, 2026 CIBO forum, asked whether a warrant is required to detain someone for ICE, he said the office should check for detainers and enforce them, citing H.B. 10 and H.B. 318: "They're supposed to work more closely with ICE. That's not happening now" ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/buncombe-sheriff-hopefuls-spar-over-190050865.html)). No statement found on the Fusus network, Flock, or plate readers; searched Aug 17, Sept 17 and Oct 7, 2026. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/gary-parris-wins-gop-nomination-buncombe-county-sheriff-face-democratic-incumbent-quentin-miller-november-general))

## Sheriffs across Western North Carolina

The same ballot decides who runs the cameras next door. **Henderson:** [[Lowell Griffin]] (R), unopposed; 13 Flock readers, a sharing list of 2,048 agencies including the FBI, and WNC's only 287(g) jail agreement. **Haywood:** open seat, [[Mark Mease]] (R) vs [[Tyler Howell]] (D), after Sheriff [[Bill Wilke]] lost his primary. Both spoke to Smoky Mountain News on Oct 7, 2026: Mease, "If it's not something the people of Haywood County want, it's not something we're going to have"; Howell, in the paper's paraphrase, that the cameras can be abused and need policy and accountability. Both would honor ICE detainers ([Smoky Mountain News](https://smokymountainnews.com/news/news-latest/mease-howell-vie-for-haywood-sheriff/)). **Madison:** [[Buddy Harwood]] (D) vs [[Robin Lyles]] (R), a rematch; the sheriff's office has refused most records requests on its cameras, as reported in June 2026 ([[WNC/Madison County/index|Madison County]]). **Rutherford:** [[Aaron Ellenburg]] (R), apparently unopposed. Settled in the primaries with no November race: Jackson ([[Doug Farmer]]), Macon ([[Brent Holbrooks]]), Transylvania ([[Chuck Owenby]]), McDowell ([[Nathan Mace]]). Commission seats that matter: Macon District 1 ([[John Shearl]], who made the de-Flock motion) and District 2 (candidate [[Dylan Castle]], who campaigned against the cameras); Jackson's contested chairmanship ([[Mark Letson]]) and District 1 ([[Todd Bryson]]). Roster with records: [[WNC/index|Western North Carolina]].

## Put the question to them

Two questions a voter can ask at a forum, by email, or at the door:

1. *"Would you vote for an ordinance requiring council approval and annual public reporting for all surveillance technology, yes or no?"* (The [CCOPS](https://www.aclu.org/community-control-over-police-surveillance) model ordinance, adopted in other cities.)
2. *"The final Axon contract wasn't public before the May 12 vote. Will you commit to releasing it and the RTIC's quarterly audits?"*

Get an answer? Send it with a link, date, and context via [[Get Involved]] and we'll add it here, favorable or not.
