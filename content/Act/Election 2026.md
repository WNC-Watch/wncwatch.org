The officials who voted on the [[What Is the RTIC|RTIC]] and the sheriff who runs the county camera network are on the November 3, 2026 ballot. This page records each candidate's votes and statements, with the date a position was last looked for where none was found. If a candidate goes on record after the last update, [[Get Involved|tell us]] with a link or recording.

([NC State Board of Elections](https://www.ncsbe.gov/voting/upcoming-election)) Asheville's municipal races moved to even years; the primary was held March 3, 2026.

## Mayor

**[[Kim Roney]]**. The record: sole no vote on the Fusus interlocal (Jan 2023), the first RTIC grant ([[2025-03-25 City Council|Mar 2025]]), and the RTIC/Axon package (May 2026). Submitted 29 written questions to APD in March 2025; no public answers had been posted as of Sept 8, 2026. Consulted the ACLU-NC and EFF. On Aug 25, 2026 she moved the Flock termination resolution, which passed 5 to 2, and then moved a moratorium on new plate reader purchases during the city's review, which failed for lack of a second ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]). At the May 28 debate: concerns about "a billionaire-owned, AI-fueled, for-profit tech company... working with ICE and Homeland Security and also the police department to surveil people without a warrant." On the May 12 process: "21 people were sent home without the opportunity to speak." ([BPR debate coverage](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance) · [campaign site](https://kimroney4asheville.com/))

**[[Esther Manheimer]]** (incumbent, seeking a 4th term). The record: voted for the Fusus interlocal (Jan 24, 2023), the first RTIC grant (Mar 25, 2025), and the RTIC grant and Axon contract (May 12, 2026), and presided over each meeting. At the May 28, 2026 debate she framed the $1.14M as fiscal efficiency, since the city already pays the Sheriff's Office for Fusus access and the grant lets Asheville "pay for our own," and answered the transparency criticism: "That's not a transparency issue. That's a functioning issue." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance)) On Aug 19, 2026 she called for terminating the Flock contract and removing the cameras: "I have heard clearly from Asheville residents" ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-mayor-esther-manheimer-vote-end-city-flock-safety-contract-remove-cameras-backlash-automatic-license-plate-readers-data-surveillance)). On Aug 25 she voted for the termination, which passed 5 to 2, and confirmed from the dais that the Axon contract covers stationary plate readers: "we did, that is one of the things that we voted for. But we haven't put them up" ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the record]]). What remains pending after the vote is on [[Getting Flock Out]]. ([campaign site](https://manheimerformayor.com/))

## Sheriff: the county camera network is on the ballot

**[[Quentin Miller]]** (D, incumbent, seeking a third term). Built the county's Fusus real-time intelligence center: ~1,800–2,000 cameras including ~1,500 in county schools. Publicly credits it for crime reductions. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/crime-takes-a-dive-in-buncombe-county-sheriff-credits-community-connection-innovative-technology-north-carolina-state-bureau-of-investigations-annual-crime-report-real-time-intelligence-center)) Also on the record: Miller has also taken documented stands *limiting* federal reach: questioning ICE detainers on constitutional grounds and barring ICE from county schools without court orders. ([Bolts](https://boltsmag.org/buncombe-county-district-attorney-sheriff-elections-and-state-preemption/)) The county network has no oversight body outside the sheriff's office ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]).

**[[Gary Parris]]** (R, Navy veteran, former BCSO). No public position found on Fusus, the camera network, or plate readers (as of Aug 17, 2026). ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/gary-parris-wins-gop-nomination-buncombe-county-sheriff-face-democratic-incumbent-quentin-miller-november-general))

## Sheriffs across Western North Carolina

The same ballot decides who runs the cameras next door. **Henderson:** [[Lowell Griffin]] (R), unopposed; 13 Flock readers, a sharing list of 2,048 agencies including the FBI, and WNC's only 287(g) jail agreement. **Haywood:** open seat, [[Mark Mease]] (R) vs [[Tyler Howell]] (D), after Sheriff [[Bill Wilke]] lost his primary; no public position on the cameras found for either as of Sept 8, 2026. **Madison:** [[Buddy Harwood]] (D) vs [[Robin Lyles]] (R), a rematch; the sheriff's office has refused most records requests on its cameras, as reported in June 2026 ([[WNC/Madison County/index|Madison County]]). **Rutherford:** [[Aaron Ellenburg]] (R), apparently unopposed. Settled in the primaries with no November race: Jackson ([[Doug Farmer]]), Macon ([[Brent Holbrooks]]), Transylvania ([[Chuck Owenby]]), McDowell ([[Nathan Mace]]). Commission seats that matter: Macon District 1 ([[John Shearl]], who made the de-Flock motion) and District 2 (candidate [[Dylan Castle]], who campaigned against the cameras); Jackson's contested chairmanship ([[Mark Letson]]) and District 1 ([[Todd Bryson]]). Roster with records: [[WNC/index|Western North Carolina]].

## The rest of the ballot

- **District Attorney**: Todd Williams, who wrote a letter supporting the RTIC, is retiring. **[[Martin Moore]]** won the decisive Democratic primary and will be Buncombe's first Black DA. No public position found on the RTIC (as of Aug 17, 2026). ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-03-06/martin-moore-to-be-buncombe-countys-first-black-district-attorney))
- **County Commission** (funds the Sheriff's Fusus network, and holds more levers over it than most people assume: [[What the Law Lets the County Do]]). D1: Anna Stearns (D) vs. Rob Stetson (R). D2 (open): Lonnie Israel (D) vs. Greg Parks (R). D3: Al Whitesides (D, i) unopposed. No surveillance positions found for any candidate (as of Aug 17, 2026).
- **US House, NC-11**: **[[Chuck Edwards]], who earmarked the RTIC's $1.14M, withdrew August 5** after a House Ethics Committee report. The GOP substituted state Rep. **[[Jennifer Balkcom]]**; she faces Democrat **[[Jamie Ager]]**. Neither has a public surveillance record (as of Aug 17, 2026). ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-08-10/state-rep-jennifer-balkcom-to-replace-rep-chuck-edwards-on-nc-11-ballot) · [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/chuck-edwards-withdraws-nc-11-congressional-district-race-jamie-ager-election-impact-midterm))
- **NC General Assembly (Buncombe)**. House 114: Eric Ager (D, i). House 115: Lindsey Prather (D, i) vs. Anthony Penland (R). House 116: Brian Turner (D, i). Senate 49: Julie Mayfield (D, i) vs. C.J. Domingo (R). The legislature just made highway plate readers permanent statewide, with every one of these four voting for the budget that did it; all four voted against the ICE-cooperation overrides. No Buncombe legislator has a found public position on plate readers (as of Aug 22, 2026). Every WNC legislator's roll calls, with links: [[WNC Legislators]].

## Put the question to them

Two questions a voter can ask at a forum, by email, or at the door:

1. *"Would you vote for an ordinance requiring council approval and annual public reporting for all surveillance technology, yes or no?"* (The [CCOPS](https://www.aclu.org/community-control-over-police-surveillance) model ordinance, adopted in other cities.)
2. *"The final Axon contract wasn't public before the May 12 vote. Will you commit to releasing it and the RTIC's quarterly audits?"*

Get an answer? Send it with a link, date, and context via [[Get Involved]] and we'll add it here, favorable or not.
