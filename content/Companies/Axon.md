Axon Enterprise is the company Asheville actually married. Flock's contract ends in February 2027 or sooner; Axon's runs to 2033 ([[What Axon Contracts Do]]). Axon owns Fusus, the platform under both the county's and the city's real-time centers ([[What Is the RTIC]]); it sells the body cameras, the in-car cameras, the tasers, the drones, and the cloud they all report to; and since 2025 it sells fixed plate readers of its own, which is what the city's August 2026 staff memo means by "expanding and integrating Axon's automated license plate reader capabilities" ([[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing]]). The contract record is on [[What Axon Contracts Do]]; the dated incidents are on [[Stories That Check Out]]. This page is the company.

## The company

- **Founded** 1993 as TASER International by [[Rick Smith]] (CEO) and Thomas Smith; renamed Axon in 2017; **headquarters** Scottsdale, Arizona; NASDAQ: AXON, an S&P 500 company. President: Josh Isner ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_Enterprise)).
- **Money:** 2025 revenue about $2.78 billion; Q1 2026 $807 million (up 34%); **Q2 2026 $904 million, up 35% year over year** ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/newsroom/press-releases/axon-reports-q2-2026-revenue-of-904-million-up-35-year-over-year)). The growth model is the bundle: every device feeds Axon Evidence, every agency is a subscription, and renewal pricing is set when the customer is locked in ([[What Axon Contracts Do]]).
- **What it bought:** Fusus (real-time crime center software, 2024, $240 million, [[What Is the RTIC]]); Dedrone (counter-drone, 2024); Sky-Hero (tactical indoor drones; the county sheriff trains on its Loki MKII, [[Buncombe County]]); Carbyne (911 technology, November 2025, $625 million); a partnership with Skydio for drone-as-first-responder. The "unified AI platform" announced April 7, 2026 ties them together: Axon Assistant for queries and reports, Axon Vision to "analyze live feeds" from fixed and body cameras and Fusus for "observable activity," Axon 911 for call data ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/newsroom/announcements/deep-dive-axon-unified-ai-platform)).

## The plate readers that blend in

Axon's fixed readers are the product Asheville's exit is heading toward.

- **Axon Outpost**: "a rugged, fixed camera that unifies livestreaming, ALPR, and vehicle attribute recognition into one device," deployable "on existing poles, new poles, buildings, vehicles, trailers, or trees," with "solar or 110V power"; agencies "share at your discretion" ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/products/axon-outpost)).
- **Axon Lightpost**: the same capability built into a streetlight fixture through Ubicquia's UbiHub, "installs in under an hour on existing light poles," "designed to capture multiple lanes at high distances, day and night, at highway speeds." Marketing line: "Deploy in minutes. Detect and stream in real time." ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/products/axon-lightpost))
- Both feed Fusus, which "brings live video and plate reads... together with other sensors and data."

404 Media, reporting on the cities that dropped Flock and immediately adopted Axon in August 2026: Axon "advertises AI-powered cameras that attach to an existing streetlamp and are designed to completely blend in with their surroundings," alongside readers "installed inside police officers' patrol vehicles" ([404 Media, Aug 6, 2026](https://www.404media.co/cities-are-ditching-flock-immediately-replacing-it-with-axon-license-plate-readers/)). Flock's cameras are visible enough to map; DeFlock's volunteers have located more than 100,000. A reader inside a streetlight is not. For a city whose council is being told removal is a reset, the difference between a camera people can see and one they cannot is the whole question of consent.

Asheville's own timeline: the contract emails released August 20 show a separate agreement for **20 Axon Fleet in-car plate readers** in its "final signature stage" in July 2026, never announced to council, and an Axon sales footer on every email: "Request a demo of our Fixed ALPR here!" ([[The RTIC Policy]]). Fixed Axon readers were pre-authorized in the May 12 package ([[Getting Flock Out]]).

## Facial recognition, drones, and the ethics board

- **Facial recognition.** Axon paused facial recognition on body cameras in 2019. On December 8, 2025 it announced "it's time to evaluate facial recognition in the field responsibly and transparently," an "early-stage field research" pilot with Edmonton Police matching faces against "a predetermined list of individuals relevant to safety or investigative needs," with results treated as "fallible" ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/newsroom/announcements/exploring-the-future-of-responsible-facial-recognition)). Asheville's Policy 1703 bans RTIC personnel from using facial recognition tools; it says nothing about what the vendor's platform does upstream ([[The RTIC Policy]] · [[Promise Tracker]]).
- **The ethics board.** In June 2022, after Axon announced plans for taser-armed drones in schools over its own AI ethics board's unanimous objection, nine of the board's members resigned; the company paused the plan ([Wikipedia, with contemporaneous sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_Enterprise)). Axon now cites an "Ethics and Equity Advisory Council" as its governance body ([Axon newsroom](https://www.axon.com/news)).
- **Draft One.** AI-written police reports from body-camera audio, marketed as saving "30-40%" of report time; independent reviews found the system can hallucinate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_Enterprise)).
- **The data program.** Customers are enrolled by default in Axon's Customer Experience Improvement Program, which licenses their data for product development; Asheville opted out by a sales rep's email, not a contract term ([[What Axon Contracts Do]] · [[Records Center|request 15]]).
- **Other record:** 59 settled wrongful-death and injury cases over tasers; promotion of the contested "excited delirium" diagnosis; a 2023 antitrust class action by three cities over body-camera pricing; the FTC's challenge to the VieVu acquisition, withdrawn in 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_Enterprise)).

## Who sells it here

The Axon account executive whose footer advertised fixed plate readers, the Sourcewell cooperative contract the city buys through, and the April 28, 2026 work session where Axon and Flock representatives were flown in to pitch council are documented on [[2026-04-28 Work Session]] and [[What Axon Contracts Do]]. The private policy workgroup that wrote the RTIC's rules met with no critics present and with the county sheriff, an Axon customer since 2023, at the table ([[The RTIC Policy]]).

## What this means in Asheville

The council's Flock vote is a vendor change inside an Axon contract. What would make it more than that: a separate, public vote before any fixed Axon reader is installed; a published sharing list and retention number for Axon plate data; a location map, since the hardware is designed not to be noticed; and the executed contract itself, which the public has still not seen ([[Getting Flock Out|the eight demands]]).
