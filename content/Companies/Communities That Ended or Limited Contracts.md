Each row below is a decision by a public body or an agency, dated, with what it did and the source. The count of communities that have dropped Flock Safety systems depends on the tracker: DeFlock's cancellation tracker and State of Surveillance put it between 50 and 102 across 27 or more states as of August 2026 ([DeFlock The USA](https://deflocktheusa.com/cancellations/) · [State of Surveillance](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/flock-safety-cancel-wave-30-cities-alpr-surveillance-contracts-2026/)); Secure Justice, an Oakland advocacy group, put it at 214 cities and counties since 2021 by early September 2026, about 90 of them in August ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/privacy/flock-cameras-us-cities-record-terminations/) · [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6053849-flock-contract-cancellations-grow-data-concerns/)). Flock says new customers outnumber departures ([AP, Aug 16, 2026](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/16/flock-safety-tightens-safeguards-as-states-cities-/)). Rows marked **NC** are in North Carolina.

## The state actions, August 26 to September 1, 2026

- **The U.S. Senate, August 26.** Senator Josh Hawley, chairing the Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism, opened an investigation into how Flock collects, retains and shares data, with documents due September 8. From the letter: "Your company's internal policies are, in practice, the only safeguards for hundreds of millions of Americans." ([the letter](https://www.hawley.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-08-26-Hawley-Letter-to-Flock-CEO.pdf) · [[Garrett Langley]])
- **Texas, August 27.** Governor Greg Abbott ordered state agencies to halt funding for Flock cameras, as the Texas Tribune prepared to report that a state board had put at least $30 million in grants into the state's Flock network, drawn from a $1 fee on auto insurance policies passed in 2023. The order followed charges against a Lufkin officer on 100 counts of misusing public information; at least six Texas departments had announced officers on leave, under investigation or charged in the prior month. ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-greg-abbott-flock-cameras-order-state-money/))
- **Arizona, August 27.** Attorney General Kris Mayes announced a statewide review of Flock and similar systems, with a report to the legislature and governor; cities switching from Flock to Axon fall under the same review. ([AZ Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/08/27/ag-mayes-to-examine-flock-surveillance-cameras-and-arizonans-privacy-rights/) · [azfamily](https://www.azfamily.com/2026/08/27/arizona-ag-announces-review-flock-safety-other-plate-readers-across-state/))
- **Florida, August 31.** The Department of Transportation revoked every permit for license plate readers in state rights-of-way and ended new approvals, citing a "recent exponential increase" in deployments and "concerning reports of misuse, data privacy concerns, and surveillance schemes"; agencies have 30 days to remove the equipment. Cameras on county roads, city streets and private property are not covered. The Jacksonville Sheriff's Office ended plate reader use throughout its jurisdiction; Tampa and several counties removed the state-road cameras and kept the rest. ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/florida-bans-flock-license-plate-readers-state-highways-rcna595420) · [WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/law-justice/2026-09-01/fdot-revokes-permits-for-flock-cameras-installed-on-state-land-next-to-roads)) In North Carolina a camera on state right-of-way sits under an NCDOT encroachment agreement; which of a county's cameras are on state right-of-way, under what agreement, is a records question no WNC jurisdiction has answered ([[Records Center]]).

## Court cases

- **Schmidt v. Norfolk.** The Institute for Justice's Fourth Amendment challenge to Norfolk, Virginia's 175-camera Flock network is before the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals (No. 26-1227), with the ACLU, EFF, Cato and EPIC filing in support. Fourth Circuit rulings bind North Carolina. ([IJ](https://ij.org/press-release/federal-court-rejects-flock-safetys-late-bid-to-join-and-block-ijs-lawsuit-challenging-norfolks-mass-surveillance-cameras/) · [case tracker](https://deflockilm.org/norfolk-flock-lawsuit-fourth-amendment/))
- **The Supreme Court, June 2026.** The Court held that acquiring mass location history is a Fourth Amendment search, rejecting the argument that a small slice of the data is not; whether the holding reaches plate-reader archives is not yet decided. ([Ohio Capital Journal](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/07/08/are-flock-cameras-next-supreme-court-rules-against-police-using-sprawling-cell-phone-location-data/))
- **Virginia.** A suit alleges police used Flock to track one driver 526 times in four months. ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/virginia-police-used-flock-cameras-track-driver-safety-lawsuit-surveil-rcna230399))
