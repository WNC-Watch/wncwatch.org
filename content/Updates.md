## August 27, 2026: a revision in voice, site-wide

Watching council respond on Aug 25 taught us something about our own writing: the residents who moved the vote did it with information brought up from care, and the site read sharper than it needed to in places. So we revised, site-wide. Our position is now stated the way we actually hold it: our concern is not cameras, and never was. A camera used for accountability under a policy a community chose, with an audit trail someone reads, is not the problem; the problem is the absence of oversight, and the risk that arrives when local cameras join large interconnected networks nobody local controls. Retitled to match: "Their Claims vs. The Record" is now **[[Asheville RTIC Claims and the Record|Asheville's RTIC claims and the record]]**, "Oversight Theater" is now **[[Police Oversight in Asheville|Police oversight in Asheville, 1991 to 2026]]**, and "Watching the Poor" is now **[[Cameras in Public Housing|Cameras in Public Housing]]** (addresses unchanged, so links keep working). Also new on [[Communities That Ended or Limited Contracts|Communities that ended or limited camera contracts]]: the February Ring and Flock story, the clearest recent evidence that people distinguish between a camera they control and a network they don't. Every fact, source, and timestamp is unchanged.

## 2026-08-23, later: The sheriff's own audit log

A reader pointed to a [public records request on MuckRock](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/buncombe-county-4590/public-records-request-fusus-logs-211165/) that produced 28 months of the Buncombe County Sheriff's Office's Fusus access log, 337,838 entries from January 2024 to May 2026, released by the county within a day and with eight columns, including every timestamp, withheld under G.S. 132-1.4. **[[Sheriffs Audit Log|Buncombe County Sheriff's Office Fusus access log, 2024 to 2026]]** is the analysis: 58 percent of all camera viewing was on Asheville Housing Authority cameras (Pisgah View Apartments alone, 39,404 events, more than any school or county building); 95 to 99 percent of viewing was live monitoring rather than recorded playback; at least 9,617 logged hours of live watching, 5,178 of them on housing; three accounts did half of all viewing; 309 plate searches and 212 saved clips in the whole period. The page explains the method and what the withheld timestamps make impossible to know.
