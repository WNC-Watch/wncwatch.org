## 2026-08-23, night: The way out, in the contracts' own words, and the sections on the home page

The home page now lists the site's eight sections with one line each; every page's breadcrumb names its section and links back to that section's front page.


A reader asked whether these contracts can be cancelled any time on 30 days' notice. Checked against the texts: neither vendor's baseline allows termination for convenience. Axon's Sourcewell contract and Master Services agreement allow exit for Axon's uncured breach (30 days) or for non-appropriation, the latter "immediately upon notice," with hardware returned within 30 days to avoid a list-price clawback. Flock's standard terms (updated Aug 21, 2026) allow non-appropriation termination "at the end of the applicable fiscal year upon thirty (30) days' written notice." North Carolina's continuing-contract statutes (G.S. 160A-17, 153A-13) and the preaudit rule (159-28) are why the clauses exist. The exit is the budget. New section on [[What Axon Contracts Do]], a short version on [[Getting Flock Out]], a lever on [[What the Law Lets the County Do]] (and one more item for its needs-a-lawyer list), and a line for Tuesday's speakers.

## 2026-08-23, later: The sheriff's own audit log

A reader pointed to a [public records request on MuckRock](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/buncombe-county-4590/public-records-request-fusus-logs-211165/) that produced 28 months of the Buncombe County Sheriff's Office's Fusus access log, 337,838 entries from January 2024 to May 2026, released by the county within a day and with eight columns, including every timestamp, withheld under G.S. 132-1.4. **[[Sheriffs Audit Log|The Sheriff's Audit Log]]** is the analysis: 58 percent of all camera viewing was on Asheville Housing Authority cameras (Pisgah View Apartments alone, 39,404 events, more than any school or county building); 95 to 99 percent of viewing was live monitoring rather than recorded playback; at least 9,617 logged hours of live watching, 5,178 of them on housing; three accounts did half of all viewing; 309 plate searches and 212 saved clips in the whole period. The page explains the method and what the withheld timestamps make impossible to know. Cross-referenced on [[Watching the Poor]], [[Buncombe County]], [[What Is the RTIC]], [[The RTIC Policy]], [[Board of Education]], [[Ella Santos]], [[Quentin Miller]], and the [[Timeline]]. [[Records Center]] request 17 asks the county for the timestamps and the city for APD's log. Individual deputies and analysts in the file are not named on this site.

## 2026-08-22: The county network, its neighbors, and the law

The first release built from the county side of the record rather than the city's. Method note, because it matters for trusting what follows: nearly everything new here came from primary documents pulled directly (the Sheriff's Office policy manual on PowerDMS, Department of Justice forfeiture tables, Flock's own transparency portals, ICE's participating-agencies list, the county's document center, housing-authority board reports, statute text at ncleg.gov) rather than from news coverage.

**New pages**

- **[[What the Law Lets the County Do]]**: the sheriff-commissioner divide in statute, the budget and preaudit levers, the forfeiture certification, interlocal ratification, the plate-reader statute's sharing clause, the three immigration-cooperation laws passed over vetoes since 2024 (HB 10, HB 318, SB 153), who in WNC has signed with ICE, and a plain list of risks and levers. Questions that need a lawyer are marked.
- **Site Updates** (this page).

**Buncombe County, rebuilt** ([[Buncombe County]])

- The Sheriff's Office's own rulebook, archived and quoted: [Policy 217 (RTIC, April 2025)](https://public.powerdms.com/Buncombe/documents/3407385), [Policy 402 (plate readers, March 2023)](https://public.powerdms.com/Buncombe/documents/2766289), [Policy 403 (drones, revised August 15, 2026)](https://public.powerdms.com/Buncombe/documents/2771887), and the HB 10 directive. Findings: "proactive monitoring" and crowd-watching at public events are written policy; APD's Policy 1703 sharing clause is copied from the county's word for word; the plate-reader audit is done with "sworn members of the FUSUS Advisory Committee," not a community board; sharing is allowed with any "criminal justice agency" for "active criminal intelligence operations"; a "Drone as First Responder" program was added one week ago.
- Federal forfeiture payments to the Sheriff's Office by year, about $988,000 across eight reported years, from DOJ's equitable-sharing tables; the annual certification a county official must sign.
- The county's Security Camera Policy revision (May 2023) giving the Sheriff's Office off-portal access to county-building footage, no board approval required.
- HACA's March 2026 executive report: both BCSD and APD hold DVR access at public housing.
- The April 2023 downtown patrol plan and the September 2023 Axon contract added to the [[Timeline]]; fewer than 15 private downtown businesses were on the "enterprise fund" network that year.
- [[Records Center]]: request 16 (audit reports, the advisory committee, forfeiture certifications, contracts, camera counts, the drone partner).
