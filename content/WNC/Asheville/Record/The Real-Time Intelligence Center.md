The Real-Time Intelligence Center is the room and the software through which the Asheville Police Department will watch its cameras, plate readers, drones and integrated private cameras in real time, run on Axon's Fusus platform under a 7.5-year contract the city signed in August 2026. This page is the door to everything this site holds on it: the status, the documents, the money, what the officials who decide have said, what is still pending, and the pages that document each part in full. The plain-language explainer is [[What Is the RTIC]]; the position this site takes is on the [[index|homepage]].

## The money

The contract totals $7,423,161.15 from Aug 1, 2026 to Dec 31, 2033. The federal earmark pays $467,602.15 of it, the Fusus subscription; the other $6,955,559 is city money, rising about 5 percent a year from $740,000 in year one to $947,211 in year six. Most of that buys equipment APD already had, bundled from its existing Axon agreements. Before the RTIC the city paid the county $30,000 a year for Fusus access. The documented savings in the staff report total $223,022.48; the chief told council "over 4 million." The year-by-year schedule and its clauses are on [[The Axon Contract as Signed|the contract, as signed]]; the grant, the city's share, and the savings figures worked through are on [[The Grant and the Contract|the federal grant and the city's share of the contract]]. Where the county's money comes from, and the downtown institutions that asked for the center: [[Follow the Money]].

## The city's answers, checked against its documents

The city's page and its officials' statements, each set beside the documents the city itself posted or released:

- **The plate-reader safeguards** on the city page (12 officers, case numbers, quarterly audits) are the Flock program's, from the chief's Aug 14 memo on Flock. The Axon readers in use have no published user count, audit, or sharing rule ([[The Technology and Public Safety Page#Which plate readers the answers describe|which plate readers the answers describe]]).
- **The private-camera answer** reproduces the Level 1 text of Connect Asheville, the Axon-built registration site, and stops before Level 2, where an owner buys a device that gives APD "direct access to your camera feed" ([[The Technology and Public Safety Page#Wording the page shares with Axon and Flock|wording the page shares with Axon and Flock]]).
- **"Axon neither has access to the Fusus or to the system, or retains data"** (the chief, May 12). The master agreement the city posted lets Axon access agency content for troubleshooting and enforcement, use usage data to develop "current and future Axon products," and enrolls every agency by default in a program that uses agency content to build products ([[The Technology and Public Safety Page#Who owns the data, and who else can use it|the terms, quoted]]).
- **"Explicit, unalterable boundaries"** (the city page, on Policy 1703). The policy is the chief's to revise without a council vote; two limits were removed between drafts ([[The RTIC Policy]]).
- **"Comprehensive quarterly audits"** (the city page). The policy routes the review to the chief with no duty to publish; the city refused the Flock audit log in 2025; none has been published ([[Promise Tracker]], rows 4 and 8).
- **"A grant that would help save the city money"** (the mayor, May 28). The city's share of the contract is $6,955,559 ([[The Grant and the Contract|the grant and the contract]]).
- **No fixed Axon readers** (the mayor and the city manager, Aug 25). The contract names Lightpost in APD's inventory, and the chief's Aug 14 memo plans Axon plate-reader infrastructure by February 2027 ([[The Axon Contract as Signed#Lightpost|Lightpost]]).

## Who decides, in their words

Each line is dated and links to the page that holds the full record and the source. Council authorized the contract 6 to 1 on May 12, 2026 and terminated the Flock contract 5 to 2 on Aug 25, 2026; four of the six yes votes of May 12 are on the November 3, 2026 ballot ([[Election 2026]]).

- **[[Esther Manheimer]]**, mayor, on the ballot. May 28, 2026, on cost: "we received a grant from the federal government to be able to pay for our own." Aug 25, 2026, on fixed Axon readers: "We have a contract where Axon will provide them if we tell them we want them." On the vendors: "Axon has what's called a closed system or what I'm calling a closed system." Declining to second a moratorium on new plate readers the same night: "I've got enough faith in the manager to do what we just talked about."
- **[[Kim Roney]]**, council member, running for mayor; the only no on every camera vote since 2023. Aug 20, 2026: "if we cancel the Flock contract and replace [it] with Axon's same functioning equipment, then we still have a violation of our constitutional rights problem." Her moratorium motion on new plate readers that night failed for lack of a second. Her 29 written questions of March 2025 have no published answers.
- **[[Bo Hess]]**, council member, chair of the Public Safety Committee, not on the ballot until 2028. March 25, 2025, on the first RTIC grant: the center "already exists" and the money buys "basic infrastructure like desk, chairs, workstations." Aug 20, 2026: "RTIC is a real-time, emergency-activated system... This is not general surveillance." Aug 25, 2026: "ending one [vendor's] contract is not the same as rejecting responsible technology." His committee has received no RTIC audit or post-approval briefing across seven recorded meetings ([[The Committee]]).
- **[[Sage Turner]]**, council member, not on the ballot until 2028. Aug 20, 2026, on Axon's plate readers: "maybe that's a part of the contract we wish wasn't there." Her May 12 yes was conditioned on the Flock cameras coming down; on Aug 17 she wrote that they remained up ([[Promise Tracker]], row 11).
- **[[Maggie Ullman]]**, council member, on the ballot; seconded both May 12 motions. Aug 20, 2026, on ending the Axon contract: "I can't fathom pulling back on our transparency tools." Aug 25, 2026, to the manager: "have we signed a contract with [Axon] for [ALPRs]?" The mayor answered that the contract provides them on request, with none installed. Sept 16, 2026: "unchecked surveillance that is networked nationally to companies we don't know is very concerning."
- **[[Antanette Mosley]]**, vice mayor, on the ballot; voted no on the Flock termination. Aug 25, 2026, on the termination with the Axon contract standing: "what we're attempting to do quite frankly is a bait and switch, and I think we're not giving our citizens credit for seeing that." Sept 16, 2026: "I happen to not believe that we have to choose between public safety and civil liberties. I think we can do both."
- **[[Sheneika Smith]]**, council member, on the ballot; voted no on the Flock termination. March 25, 2025: suggested a community forum on the center; none has been held.
- **[[D.K. Wesley]]**, city manager, signed the contract Aug 4, 2026. May 7, 2026, five days before the vote, on the contract: it "does not exist yet." Aug 25, 2026, on fixed Axon readers: "we will not put those up until that community conversation happens." She runs the ten-item review ordered Aug 25 and names the permanent chief.
- **[[Jackie Stepp]]**, interim chief, signed as department director July 27, 2026. May 12, 2026: "Trust will not be assumed. It will be demonstrated." The same night, on the vendor: "Axon neither has access to the Fusus or to the system, or retains data." Aug 14, 2026, in her memo to council: APD is "expanding and integrating Axon's ALPR capabilities." To the policy workgroup, June 11, 2026: the DOJ grant contains "standard language that local law enforcement will cooperate with federal law enforcement investigations."
- **[[Quentin Miller]]**, sheriff, on the ballot, runs the county network the city's center connects to. To the workgroup, per APD's notes: the RTIC is "a tool used only when requested," and audits "serve internal purposes rather than the public directly." His challenger [[Gary Parris]] pledged in February 2026 a "Unified Operations Intelligence Center to share actionable intelligence with all agencies."
- **The council candidates.** [[Jeffrey Burroughs]], May 14, 2026: "If I had been on council, I would have voted no." [[Drew Ball]], Sept 16, 2026: "We need to make sure we control our data so it's not fed to a national network. We need closed loop systems that we control." [[Keith Young]], Sept 16, 2026: "Can we use this system to make our public safety system stronger? Yes, but should it be the one that they agreed upon? Probably not."
- **[[Chuck Edwards]]**, U.S. representative through January 2027, signed the earmark request May 22, 2024. No statement on the center since the April 2026 debate has been found.
