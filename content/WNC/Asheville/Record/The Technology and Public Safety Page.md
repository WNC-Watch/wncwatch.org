The City of Asheville published a "Technology and Public Safety" page in August 2026 ([publicinput.com/f65124](https://publicinput.com/f65124)), with frequently asked questions on the Real-Time Intelligence Center, body-worn cameras, license plate readers and drones, a timeline, a question box, and links to the contracts. Interim Chief Stepp's Aug 14, 2026 memo announced it, and at the Aug 25, 2026 meeting Kim Roney thanked staff for "contracts being uploaded to the new Technology dashboard." The page is the city's first single public account of these systems, and it carries the executed Axon contract, which this site had listed as unreleased until Sept 23 ([[Corrections]]). This page sets the city's statements beside the record so a resident can read both before a meeting. Quotations are from the page as read on Sept 23, 2026; the city says it will revise the answers from questions received, so the live page may differ. The hub for everything on the center: [[The Real-Time Intelligence Center]].

## What the page puts on the record

- The executed Axon contract, City of Asheville Contract 92700065: dated Aug 1, 2026, signed by the chief July 27 and the city manager Aug 4, 2026, running to Dec 31, 2033, $7,423,161.15 in total ([[The Axon Contract as Signed|the contract, as signed]]).
- The Flock contract, City of Asheville Contract 92500339 with Insight Public Sector, Feb 1, 2025 to Jan 31, 2027, with Flock's terms of service attached.
- The FUSUS appendix to the Sourcewell contract, and the Sourcewell master agreement.
- A statement that "Asheville Police Department has deactivated all user accounts and access to the Flock system," the first city statement on APD's Flock accounts ([[Getting Flock Out]], demand 3).
- Working links to both plate-reader portals.
- APD's own account, in its March 2026 committee slides, that the federal earmark began when "APD was invited to complete a questionnaire provided by Rep. Edwards' office" in 2024, with the city manager's office authorizing the response May 22, 2024 ([[Chuck Edwards]]).

## Happening now

**The page.** "Staff is in the process of planning opportunities for the community to engage on this topic. Engagement opportunities are anticipated to occur in late 2026 or early 2027."

**The record.** APD's policy workgroup notes of July 23, 2026 set the schedule: staff training after the policy is released, APD "will begin to solicit integration in the Fall of 2026," full operation in spring 2027 ([[The RTIC Policy]]). The contract bills the Fusus subscription from Dec 1, 2026. The Aug 25, 2026 resolution ordered the city manager to review ten items, including "an independent civil-liberties policy reviewer," with no date; the chief's Aug 14 memo says APD is "beginning conversations with a civil liberties expert." The city manager told council Aug 25 that no stationary Axon plate readers go up "until that community conversation happens" ([[2026-08-25 City Council]]). The page does not mention the review, the reviewer, or the assurance.

**The page.** "The timing and process of removal of the stationary Flock ALPR cameras and related equipment are dependent on Flock."

**The record.** Flock's terms, attached to the posted contract (clause 7.2): on termination Flock "will remove any applicable Flock Hardware at a commercially reasonable time period." No date has been published. In Evanston, Illinois, terminated cameras were reinstalled and the last came down after a press inquiry ([[Getting Flock Out]]). On Aug 25 staff said covering the lenses was not planned but "we can evaluate that as an option" (minutes).

## The Real-Time Intelligence Center

**The page.** "The Real-Time Intelligence Center (RTIC) is a centralized hub that integrates technologies, such as body-worn cameras, Automatic License Plate Readers (ALPRs), and drones, into a single, coordinated interface. By utilizing these components through the Fusus platform, the RTIC provides actionable intelligence to support officers during active calls, investigations, and open cases."

**The record.** The same description, with private cameras added, is on [[What Is the RTIC]]. The FUSUS appendix posted with the contract sets the product tiers: from 150 managed end points and no video streams (Lite) to 4,500 end points and 4,500 connected streams (Enterprise Plus). Which tier the city bought is in the Axon quote the contract references (Q-808220-46198AS), which is not posted.

**The page.** "No, the RTIC does not utilize facial recognition technology."

**The record.** Resolution 26-97 (May 12, 2026) and Policy 1703, section 1.G: "RTIC personnel will not use tools or platforms that use facial recognition technology." The ban entered the policy in the July 9, 2026 draft; the June 25 and June 29 drafts did not have it. The policy is the chief's to revise; the workgroup notes record Stepp's statement that any future use "would require a return to Council for approval," a commitment that is not in the policy text ([[The RTIC Policy]] · [[Promise Tracker]], row 1). The Aug 25 resolution's Section 4 bars linking the city's Flock data to any outside facial-recognition database; it says nothing about Axon Fusus. The posted contract and the Sourcewell master agreement neither authorize nor prohibit facial recognition ([[What Axon Contracts Do]]).

**The page.** "No, the RTIC is not used for passive surveillance of civilian activity. Integration with private security cameras is entirely voluntary and requires explicit permission from business owners or residents." "Registering your cameras does not allow the Asheville Police Department access to your live video stream - it only enables investigators to know a camera is present at your location."

**The record.** The registration site the page links, connectasheville.org ("Powered by Axon," copyright Axon Enterprise, Inc.), offers two levels. Level 1 is registration, as the page describes. Level 2, "Integrate Your Cameras," is the purchase of "a small CORE device to provide police with direct access to camera feeds during emergencies." The page's FAQ describes Level 1 only. APD's workgroup notes say the department "will begin to solicit integration in the Fall of 2026." What integration produces in practice is on the record for the county's Fusus network, whose policy language APD copied: the Sheriff's Office's own access log, January 2024 to May 2026, shows 217,506 live camera views against 7,052 recorded views, 58 percent of them on public housing ([[Sheriffs Audit Log|the Sheriff's Office Fusus access log]]). The terms an owner accepts at Level 2 are on [[Before You Connect Your Cameras]].

**The page.** "The Asheville Police Department has established a multi-layered governance framework that restricts system access, mandates justification for every query, and enforces strict routine oversight. APD ensures strict policy compliance through comprehensive quarterly audits of all system and network activity, backed by a zero-tolerance policy..."

**The record.** Policy 1703, section 4.D, as released in draft: "At least quarterly, the Professional Standards Section will randomly review user access logs within the Axon Fusus system," with documentation "submitted to the chief of police or designee." Nothing in the policy requires publication or a report to council. The policy is not posted on the page; the page says it "will be ... published on our transparency portal" (timeline entry marked live). A resident's July 2025 request for the Flock audit logs was refused Sept 9, 2025 under G.S. 20-183.31(b) and G.S. 132-1.4 ([[Records Center]]). Sheriff Miller told the workgroup on June 11, 2026 that audits "serve internal purposes rather than the public directly" ([[The RTIC Policy]]). The Public Safety Committee's Sept 24, 2026 agenda carries no audit item ([[The Committee]]). No audit report or summary has been published as of Sept 23, 2026.

## License plate readers

**The page.** "The Asheville Police Department (APD) utilizes Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) technology provided by Flock Safety strictly as an objective, crime-solving investigative tool. Recognizing public concern regarding government surveillance and data privacy, APD has established one of the most restrictive and heavily audited ALPR operational policies in the region."

**The record.** The page's first box says APD's Flock access has been deactivated. No comparison of APD's policy with other agencies' has been published. The written policy state law requires, APD Policy 505, is on PowerDMS ([[Documents]]) and is not linked from the page. The county's policy, which APD's RTIC rulebook copies in part, is on [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Buncombe County]].

**The page.** "Data that does not match a specific investigation is automatically purged after 30 days." (Stated four times on the page.)

**The record.** The chief's Aug 14, 2026 memo: retention "reduced ... from 30 days to 21 days." The chief on Aug 25 (minutes): "Flock is currently 21 days and anything beyond 21 days will be automatically purged." The Axon portal on Sept 23, 2026: "Plate Read Record Retention 21 Days," "Hotlist Hit Record Retention 21 Days." The same portal: search reasons and case numbers are "captured in audit logs and stored indefinitely." Data exported to evidence.com for a case stays under the records schedule (the chief, Aug 25). Policy 1703 sets no retention period ([[The RTIC Policy]]).

**The page.** "The program is restricted to 12 authorized officers, with every search requiring a verifiable criminal case or incident number." "Authorized users were reduced by 33% (from 18 to 12 officers)."

**The record.** The count is the Flock program's (the Aug 14 memo). The page gives no count of who holds Axon ALPR or Fusus access, and Policy 1703 sets no cap: access is whoever the chief authorizes. The Axon portal's own policy line: "All data searches require a valid search reason or case number." In Mooresville, 31 searches each carried a logged reason and passed review before the reasons were alleged to be false; EFF's review of 11.4 million Flock searches found more than 14 percent justified with the single word "investigation" ([[Camera Network Misuse|Camera network misuse, documented cases]]).

**The page.** "The technology is not used for real-time tracking of citizens." "APD's ALPR cameras capture a single, point-in-time photograph of a vehicle's exterior ... They do not stream live video or track individual routines." "There are 11 stationary ALPR cameras and 162 cameras installed in APD marked fleet vehicles."

**The record.** The Axon portal, Sept 23, 2026: 835,194 plate reads retained over the 21-day window from 139 active in-car readers (0 Outpost, 0 Lightpost), with hotlist alerts "in patrol vehicles" in real time, 395 hits retained. The portal listed 150 vehicles in February 2025 and 137 on Aug 26, 2026 ([[Corrections]]). What a set of dated plate reads can show about a person over 21 days is on [[What They Can Actually Learn About You|What a plate read becomes]].

**The page.** "Data is not shared with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Data ownership belongs 100% to APD. External sharing occurs exclusively with vetted law enforcement agencies under strict, reciprocal agreements that APD can revoke at any time."

**The record.** Flock's terms, attached to the posted contract, clause 4.1: the city "grants to Flock a limited, non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable, worldwide license to use the Customer Data"; clause 4.3: Flock may anonymize the data for "the Services and related systems ... and other Flock offerings"; clause 5.3: Flock "may access, use, preserve and/or disclose the Footage to law enforcement authorities, government officials, and/or third parties, if legally required to do so or if Flock has a good faith belief" that it is reasonably necessary. Through August 2026 APD's plate data was shared within a 500-mile radius; the Aug 14 memo narrows it to Tennessee, South Carolina, Virginia and Georgia. The APD Flock Camera Sheet released in 2025 lists the agencies with search and hot-list access ([[Documents]]). Policy 1703 does not mention immigration or ICE; its sharing clause allows disclosure "as permitted by law or with a compelling need to share such information for legitimate law enforcement purposes" ([[The RTIC Policy]]). The chief told the workgroup June 11, 2026 that the DOJ grant "contains no language on immigration, aside from standard language that local law enforcement will cooperate with federal law enforcement investigations." In the cases documented nationally, immigration searches of a local network were run by another agency in the network ([[Camera Network Misuse|Camera network misuse, documented cases]]). For Axon Fusus, the posted FUSUS appendix: "Axon may collect, use, transfer, disclose and otherwise process Customer Content in the context of ... monitoring the Customer's use of FUSUS systems, and undertaking data analytics," and Customer Content "may not be distributed by Axon to any third parties outside of the Customer's organization without the Customer's expressed written consent."

**The page.** "Flock does not own, sell, or monetize APD data."

**The record.** Clause 4.1 of the attached terms: "Flock does not own and shall not sell Customer Data." Clause 4.3 grants the anonymized-data license quoted above. Axon states it does not sell personal data and nothing in the record contradicts that; the documented issues are default programs, metadata control, and lock-in terms ([[Axon]]). The city's opt-out from Axon's Customer Experience Improvement Program rests on a sales representative's July 16, 2026 email; Axon's own appendix says it confirms withdrawal within 30 days, and no confirmation has been posted ([[The RTIC Policy]]).

**The page.** "Every search requires a documented criminal case number and generates an unalterable digital audit trail." "APD conducts transparent, quarterly audits of all system activity."

**The record.** The audit trail exists; the city refused to release it in September 2025 ([[Promise Tracker]], row 8). No audit has been published. The first quarterly window under Policy 1703 closes about October 2026 (row 4).

**The page.** "Policy explicitly prohibits monitoring lawful demonstrations, political gatherings, or civil infractions."

**The record.** Policy 1703's June 25 draft authorized the RTIC to "utilize available technological resources near relevant events to look for suspicious activity leading up to and during the event"; the final draft says "assisting in planning for public events, large crowds, and special operations" ([[The RTIC Policy]]). The county's Policy 217, from which APD's sharing clause is copied, keeps the event language. Which APD policy contains the demonstration prohibition the page describes is not stated; Policy 505 governs plate readers and is not linked.

## Drones

**The page.** "The Asheville Police Department and Asheville Fire Department established their respective drone programs in 2022." "Drones are not used for continuous, routine, or random surveillance of the general public." "Data captured is relevant only to the specific mission authorized and is managed under strict data retention policies."

**The record.** The page's own timeline dates the programs to June 2023. No retention period is stated. APD's drones have been used in enforcement of homeless-related offenses (WLOS, on [[Asheville RTIC Claims and the Record|Asheville's RTIC claims and the record]]). The posted contract bundles the drones, and the pricebook page lists Skydio "DFR" (drone as first responder) plans; the Sheriff's Office added a drone-as-first-responder program to its Policy 403 on Aug 15, 2026 ([[Documents]]). Whether APD has bought a DFR plan is in the unposted quote.

## The timeline

**The page** lists twenty-two entries from 2015 to Aug 25, 2026.

**The record.** Entries the page does not carry, each documented on this site: the December 2022 MOU giving APD Fusus access; the Jan 24, 2023 council vote joining the county network, 4 to 1; the March 7, 2025 briefing at which council first heard of the plate readers and of access to "over 177,000 cameras"; the March 25, 2025 grant on the consent agenda, listed on the page as "City Council approved the JAG grant"; the July 2025 records refusal; the April 14, April 28 and May 7, 2026 sessions; the July 16, 2026 emails sending the contract for signature along with a second contract for 20 Axon Fleet in-car readers; the Aug 20, 2026 records release ([[WNC/Asheville/index|the Asheville timeline]]). Within the page: the drone programs are dated 2022 in the FAQ and June 2023 in the timeline; the body-worn camera rollout is complete by the end of 2016 in the FAQ and by June 2017 in the timeline; the May 2026 entry carries the text of the Aug 25, 2026 resolution's whereas clauses; the last entry is dated "August 25, 2006." The Aug 14 memo dates the civil-liberties resolution to "June 2026"; Resolution 26-97 was adopted May 12, 2026.

**The page.** "A community-based policy workgroup, made up of eight diverse stakeholders, worked with APD to develop the RTIC policy. The policy will establish explicit, unalterable boundaries on what the technology can be used for (e.g., active emergencies) and what it is strictly prohibited from doing (e.g., random, passive monitoring)."

**The record.** APD's own notes of the four sessions (June 11 to July 23, 2026) name the invited groups; no group that opposed the RTIC was invited, and the notes record the sheriff suggesting "we consider having those who are opposed join us at the table" and the chief's answer that "we believe we are accomplishing this through the tours, community meetings, etc." Between the June 29 and July 9 drafts, "Remote terminal access will not be permitted" and the once-per-shift hotlist review were removed. The policy is a department policy the chief can revise ([[The RTIC Policy]]). Its effective date was blank in the last released draft, and it is not posted on the page.

**The page.** "Sourcewell/Axon Contract, which combines all existing Asheville Police equipment contracts (cameras, Tasers, drones) with warranties, cloud storage, and new FUSUS software into one package using Sourcewell contract pricing."

**The record.** [[The Axon Contract as Signed|The contract, as signed]]. The scope sentence lists "Body-Worn Cameras, Tasers, Drones, Lightpost and Interview Room" as APD's current inventory. Lightpost is Axon's fixed plate-reader product; the portal shows 0 Lightpost devices; the mayor said Aug 25, "We do not have stationary Axon license plate readers. We have a contract where Axon will provide them if we tell them we want them." What the Lightpost line refers to is not answerable from the posted documents.

**The page.** RTIC open-house tours "every Thursday from 2:00 to 4:00 p.m., beginning June 4th."

**The record.** This site has carried the tours as every other Thursday since June 2026 ([[Promise Tracker]], row 9); the two accounts differ, and the registration line is 828-259-5881.
