## 2022 to 2023: the county's network

**June 2022: [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Buncombe County]]'s 2,000-camera network surfaces.** The Citizen Times reports that [[Quentin Miller|Sheriff Quentin Miller]]'s office is running roughly 2,000 cameras on the Fusus platform (about 1,500 inside county schools, another 500 across housing communities and businesses) feeding a real-time intelligence center most residents had never heard of. The Sheriff proposes expanding downtown along the courthouse corridor and Patton Avenue, with businesses paying $60 per camera per year to join. ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/2-000-camera-network-feeding-090126108.html))

**January 24, 2023: Asheville joins the county's Fusus network, 4 to 1.** City Council approves an interlocal agreement giving APD access to the county's Fusus center (roughly 1,800 cameras at the time) for $30,000 a year, plus city cameras at City Hall, Pritchard Park, and North Lexington. **[[Kim Roney]] casts the only no vote**. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-police-get-access-to-buncombe-county-sheriffs-office-fusus-real-time-intelligence-center-camera-system-collaboration-emergency-situations), [Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/asheville-joins-buncombe-county-surveillance-system/))

**March 22, 2023: The sheriff's plate-reader policy takes effect.** [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Policy 402]] allows sharing with any "criminal justice agency" for "active criminal intelligence operations," puts "gang and terrorist watch lists" and "authorized Buncombe County Sheriff's Office intelligence projects" on the hot list, and assigns the annual audit to the Fusus coordinator "along with sworn members of the FUSUS Advisory Committee." The policy sets no limit on federal sharing and requires no public report. ([BCSO Policy 402](https://public.powerdms.com/Buncombe/documents/2766289))

**April 2023: The sheriff's downtown patrol plan uses the cameras.** A sheriff's plan obtained by Asheville Watchdog has deputies using the Fusus network to "monitor downtown business district" and "advise assigned personnel of any suspicious or criminal activity observed during monitoring"; the idea came from commission Chair Brownie Newman, who had questioned the program ten months earlier. ([Asheville Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/down-town-document-reveals-details-of-buncombe-plan-to-assist-asheville-police/))

**September 2023: The sheriff's $2.4M Axon contract.** Five years of body cameras and dash cameras that stream live into Fusus; "whenever our deputies are in the vicinity of APD officers, the system makes all the cameras come on," per [[Quentin Miller|Sheriff Miller]]. The same report counts fewer than 15 private downtown businesses on the network sold to commissioners as a revenue opportunity. ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/buncombe-county-sheriff-gets-real-090507063.html)) [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Buncombe County]]

## 2024 to 2025: the buildout

**2024: The RTIC earmark is requested.** In April 2024, APD and the office of U.S. Rep. [[Chuck Edwards]] (R-NC-11) connect over a federal earmark for a [[What Is the RTIC|Real-Time Intelligence Center]]; accounts differ on who initiated the contact ([[Chuck Edwards|his page]] documents both versions). Edwards signs the formal request letter on **May 22, 2024**; his entire written justification is one sentence. Council is not informed. The sequence became public through the work of [Communities Against Mass Surveillance](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/).

**2024 to 2025: The plate readers go live.** Chief Stepp's August 14, 2026 memo to council dates it this way: Axon in-car plate readers on marked patrol cars "since April 2024," and "in February 2025, APD added the Flock ALPR system" ([memo](https://docs.google.com/document/d/13kwmbwt6T9PiEjUgrFNb9ZHFGzaIsHi5cvsBHkpq-lY/edit)); BPR likewise reports APD "has contracted with Flock since 2025" ([BPR, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-08-21/asheville-considers-early-termination-of-flock-but-leaves-door-open-for-other-license-plate-readers)). Earlier coverage, and this site until August 23, put the 11 Flock cameras at April 2024; the contract, which the city has not released, would settle it. Between 2024 and December 2025 the readers scan roughly **3.1 million plates**, in a city of about 95,000 people, with data shared with agencies across a **500-mile, multi-state radius**. APD also runs Axon in-car plate readers and roughly two dozen drones. ([Asheville Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/opinion-youre-on-camera-whether-you-like-it-or-not-but-asheville-needs-to-be-more-transparent-about-video-surveillance-proposal/), [Annie Dance / Cops & Congress](https://anniedance.substack.com/p/asheville-expands-regional-surveillance))

**April 9, 2025: The sheriff's RTIC policy is approved.** [[Quentin Miller|Sheriff Miller]] approves [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Policy 217]]: "proactive monitoring," cameras near public events "to look for suspicious activity leading up to and during the event," rapid-deployment cameras, and a sharing clause APD would later copy verbatim into its own Policy 1703. ([BCSO Policy 217](https://public.powerdms.com/Buncombe/documents/3407385))

**March 7, 2025: Council's first briefing on the plate readers, at [[Kim Roney|Roney]]'s request.** Deputy Chief [[Jackie Stepp]] briefs council on the LPR program for the first time, stating that mobile readers had run for nearly a year, stationary readers "approximately two years," cross-jurisdiction sharing was live, and APD had access to **"over 177,000 cameras across the Eastern US"** through Flock and Axon partnerships, "and we've had that access." Roney requested the briefing after reading about the cameras online; the committee that would normally review the program was not meeting. Full write-up with timestamps: [[2025-03-07 Agenda Briefing]].

**March 25, 2025: First RTIC grant, on the consent agenda.** A $71,999 federal grant for RTIC equipment (video wall, workstations) is placed on the consent agenda, the part of the meeting reserved for routine, undebated items. Roney pulls it for discussion and submits **29 written questions** to APD about data access, control, and constitutional implications. The questions go unanswered. It passes 6 to 1, along with six other police funding items. Council member [[Sheneika Smith]] says on the record "I kind of feel robbed of having those conversations," and suggests a community forum; it is never held. [[Bo Hess]] speaks for the item: "we're not buying surveillance, we're buying chairs", and says the center "already exists." Full write-up: [[2025-03-25 City Council]]. ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/bpr-news/2025-03-26/last-night-at-council-asheville-approves-funding-for-police-surveillance-hub), [CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

**February 2026: The $1.14M earmark arrives.** The Community Project Funding earmark secured by Rep. [[Chuck Edwards]] comes through: $1.14 million, administered by the Department of Justice. The DOJ's conditions are published only after acceptance. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

## April to May 2026: the vote

**April 11, 2026: SASSI's first public event on the RTIC.** Three days before the council vote, [[Get Involved|SASSI]] (Southerners Against Surveillance Systems & Infrastructure) holds "You Are Being Watched: An Introduction to Police Surveillance in Buncombe County" at Firestorm Books. ([SASSI](https://sassisouth.org))

**April 14, 2026: The RTIC item is pulled from the consent agenda.** More than 40 residents arrive to speak against accepting the $1.14M. The item is pulled from the agenda right before the meeting. A resident, speaking at public comment: "Let's call it what it actually is: a centralized hub for spying on the people of Asheville." [[Esther Manheimer|Mayor Manheimer]] says the issue "warrants a full conversation." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/asheville-city-council-punts-vote-on-police-tech-funding-and-suggests-property-tax-hike))

**April 27, 2026: Asheville Watchdog publishes an opinion column against the RTIC.** Asheville Watchdog's John Boyle publishes an opinion piece criticizing the city for burying the initiative on consent agendas and demanding transparency. The cost breakdown he reports: ~$400K video wall, $467K in software over 7–8 years, $250K operational. ([Asheville Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/opinion-youre-on-camera-whether-you-like-it-or-not-but-asheville-needs-to-be-more-transparent-about-video-surveillance-proposal/))

**[[2026-04-28 Work Session|April 28, 2026]]: The committee work session with Flock and Axon.** The Policy, Finance and Infrastructure Committee hears presentations from Axon and Flock sales representatives alongside Interim Chief [[Jackie Stepp]]. No public comment is allowed. CAMS members publish a [fact-check one-pager](https://www.sunshinelabs.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Asheville-RTIC-%E2%80%93-1-Pager.pdf) responding to vendor claims. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

**May 11, 2026: The Government Accountability Project publishes its position.** The Government Accountability Project of Asheville publishes its position: reject the RTIC/Axon expansion until real oversight exists: no civilian oversight body, no facial recognition ban, vendor lock-in, and no final negotiated contract available for council to review. ([GAP](https://gapavl.org/asheville-should-reject-the-proposed-rtic-axon-surveillance-expansion-until-real-oversight-and-accountability-exist/))

**May 12, 2026: Council approves the RTIC grant and Axon contract, 6 to 1.** The RTIC items are placed at the end of a budget meeting. Public comment is capped at one hour; **21 residents who had signed up do not get to speak.** Of the 23 who speak, 22 oppose. The council approves the $1.14M grant and a **7.5-year contract with Axon** (body cameras, tasers, drones, interview-room tech, and Fusus software) without the final contract text available for review. [[Kim Roney|Roney]], the only no, presents ACLU and EFF concerns. Residents chant "Shame!" as it passes. Council member [[Bo Hess]] attaches a resolution stating the tech will be used "solely for lawful purposes." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-13/asheville-council-accepts-police-tech-funding-hears-budget-proposal), [828NewsNow](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-asheville-oks-real-time-police-intelligence-center-despite-public-backlash/), [Enlace Latino NC](https://enlacelatinonc.org/en/Asheville-City-Council-approves-creation-of-real-time-intelligence-center/), [People's World](https://www.peoplesworld.org/article/surveillance-state-major-police-intelligence-center-coming-to-asheville-n-c/))

> [!note] Verified from the city's own video
> Press coverage disagreed on the vote count (4 to 1, 5 to 1, and 6 to 1 all appeared). The city's own recording settles it: the chair states on video, "the vote was **6 to 1** in favor of item B1," and B2 followed 6 to 1 as well. The B1 vote was taken **off-microphone amid shouting, seconds after public comment closed**; a council member had to announce "in case you didn't notice, there was just a vote." Full minute-by-minute reconstruction with video timestamps: [[2026-05-12 City Council]].

**May 13, 2026: The county releases the sheriff's audit log.** One day after an anonymous public records request, Buncombe County produces 28 months of the Sheriff's Office's Fusus access log, 337,838 entries, and withholds eight columns including every timestamp under G.S. 132-1.4 ([MuckRock](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/buncombe-county-4590/public-records-request-fusus-logs-211165/)). The released columns show 58 percent of camera viewing on public housing and 95 to 99 percent of it live. Analyzed August 2026: [[Sheriffs Audit Log|Buncombe County Sheriff's Office Fusus access log, 2024 to 2026]].

**May 25, 2026: CAMS holds a surveillance-awareness event.** CAMS throws a surveillance-awareness social at DayTrip: DJ sets and a Q&A with [[Benn Jordan]] (the researcher whose Flock investigations reached national press) plus Kim Roney, Colonel Panic, and Patrick Conant. ([Sunshine Labs](https://www.sunshinelabs.org/sos-sunshine-over-surveillance/))

**May 28, 2026: Surveillance at the first mayoral debate.** At the first Roney–Manheimer debate, surveillance is one of the main disagreements. Roney raises "a billionaire-owned, AI-fueled, for-profit tech company... working with ICE" surveilling without warrants; Manheimer frames the grant as cost savings. ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance))

## June 2026 onward


**June 4, 2026: APD opens RTIC public tours.** APD opens bi-weekly public tours of the RTIC space, framed as transparency "following public debates over privacy." Every other Thursday, 2–4 PM; register at 828-259-5881. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-police-department-tours-new-real-time-intelligence-center-approved-federal-funds-social-media-transparency-privacy-public-debates-analyze-data-emergencies-register))

**July 7, 2026: The state budget makes the SBI plate-reader program permanent.** The NC budget makes the SBI's highway license plate reader program permanent, authorizing ALPRs on state-maintained roads. The SBI's stated goal: a camera at every entrance and exit to the state. ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/nccapitol/surveillance-drivers-camera-alpr-license-plate-reader-nc-budget-public-safety-privacy-july-2026/), [Daily Tar Heel](https://dailytarheel.com/article/city-ai-highway-surveillance-north-carolina-budget-20260728))

**July 8, 2026: WLOS reports the RTIC's planned integrations.** A WLOS investigation details the RTIC's planned integrations (Flock ALPRs, body and car cameras, drones, voluntary private cameras in Fusus's "one pane of glass") and APD's internal controls: quarterly audits, keycard reviews, and search-term blocks. APD tech manager James Wingo: blocks cover "anything to do with immigration, anything to do with reproductive issues." The controls are administered by APD itself. Operational target: **spring/summer 2027**. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/news-13-investigates/inside-asheville-police-department-apd-future-real-time-intelligence-center-cameras-license-plate-readers-car-body-cams-drones-technology-investigations))

**June 11 – July 23, 2026: The RTIC policy workgroup meets.** Interim Chief [[Jackie Stepp|Stepp]] convenes a four-session "RTIC Stakeholder Policy Workgroup" (APD staff, Sheriff [[Quentin Miller|Miller]], and invited community members; none of the groups that opposed the RTIC were invited) to shape Policy 1703, the center's governing policy. The sessions were not announced; the meeting notes surface only through an August records request. What they show, including the stated goal of a "regional RTIC," a coming city-county MOU, and the messaging strategy ("community partnership rather than a surveillance initiative"): [[The RTIC Policy]].

**July 14, 2026: [[WNC/Macon County/index|Macon County]] ends its Flock contract, 5 to 0.** County commissioners vote **5 to 0** to end their $47,000 Flock contract; 12 cameras come down. The push came largely from Republican residents and officials. See [[Western North Carolina and the State|Western North Carolina and the state]]. ([Smoky Mountain News](https://smokymountainnews.com/news/news-latest/de-flocking-macon-county-votes-to-let-alpr-contract-lapse/))

**July 29 & August 5, 2026: Two NC officers charged over Flock searches.** A Charlotte officer becomes the first in NC criminally charged for an unauthorized Flock search; a week later a Mooresville officer is arrested for using Flock to stalk his ex-wife: 31 searches. ([WNC/state coverage via NCLocal](https://nclocal.org/2026/07/30/balancing-public-safety-and-privacy-wnc-wrestles-with-surveillance-issues/))

**August 7, 2026: A Flock camera at Carrier Park is vandalized.** A Flock camera near Carrier Park is found vandalized. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/gallery/asheville-flock-camera-vandalized-carrier-park-license-plate-reader-damaged))

**August 16–22, 2026: National Week of Action.** Asheville joins the DeFlock National Week of Action Against ALPRs: a West Asheville kickoff potluck, an online panel, and a Friday celebration at Cooperative Coffee. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org), [DeFlock](https://deflock.org))

**August 14, 2026: The chief's memo to council on Flock and Axon.** [[Jackie Stepp]] writes council that APD "is not pursuing a full transition away from Flock" but is "exploring transitioning... to a Flock software license that would preserve access to the Flock network," while "expanding and integrating Axon's automated license plate reader capabilities," with "the Axon ALPR infrastructure operational" by February 2027. The memo counts 86 Flock cameras in Buncombe County, 11 of them the city's, says the sheriff "currently uses Flock ALPR cameras," and describes evaluating "consolidating the BCSO RTIC into the APD RTIC." It becomes public as an agenda attachment a week later. ([memo](https://docs.google.com/document/d/13kwmbwt6T9PiEjUgrFNb9ZHFGzaIsHi5cvsBHkpq-lY/edit) · [[Getting Flock Out|scored against the eight demands]])

**August 19, 2026: The mayor calls for Flock termination.** Mid-Week of Action, two days after [[Sage Turner]] documented that the city's Flock cameras were still up despite her vote's conditions, [[Esther Manheimer|Mayor Manheimer]] issues a statement calling for a vote at the **August 25** council meeting to authorize city manager [[D.K. Wesley]] to terminate the Flock contract: "I have heard clearly from Asheville residents, and I'm calling on Asheville to turn off the Flock cameras and remove them, which requires terminating the Flock contract." She says a moratorium wouldn't be enough because "our contract does not allow for the removal of the technology unless we terminate the contract," and asks for a transparent policy on handling and deleting the data already collected. The statement says nothing about APD's Flock search accounts, the county's cameras, or the [[What Axon Contracts Do|Axon contract]]; see [[Getting Flock Out]] for what a full exit requires, and note it stays a statement until council votes. That same morning, hours before the announcement, APD had publicized using Flock cameras to identify suspects in a West Asheville paintball assault case. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-mayor-esther-manheimer-vote-end-city-flock-safety-contract-remove-cameras-backlash-automatic-license-plate-readers-data-surveillance) · [828newsnow](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-asheville-mayor-calls-for-vote-to-terminate-flock-camera-contract/) · [WLOS, paintball](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-flock-cameras-paintball-assaults-juvenile-suspects-west-asheville-haywood-road))

**August 20, 2026: Agenda briefing on the termination resolution.** At the agenda briefing for the Aug 25 meeting, [[Esther Manheimer|Manheimer]] presents the actual resolution and discloses two facts: **Flock changed its policy to require 10 cameras minimum for system access**, which ends the "keep one camera" plan, and removal requires termination because "the cameras are technically not ours. They're Flock's." A staff memo read aloud by [[Kim Roney|Roney]] says the city is "expanding and integrating Axon's automated license plate reader capabilities" while Flock winds down, and the mayor confirms Axon stationary readers are "in the pipeline." Full write-up: [[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing]].

**August 20, 2026: Seven RTIC policy records released.** Seven records land via two public records requests shared by [[Get Involved|Sunshine Labs]]' Patrick Conant: four drafts of Policy 1703 (facial recognition ban included, but only from the third draft on; a remote-access ban and per-shift hotlist review deleted along the way), the private policy workgroup's meeting notes, and Axon emails showing the contract went for signature **July 16**, a never-announced second contract for **20 in-car plate readers**, and the city's opt-out from Axon's default data program. The full analysis: [[The RTIC Policy]].

**August 21, 2026: The Aug 25 agenda posts, Flock resolution as New Business.** The Flock resolution is New Business item VI.A for August 25, not consent. Its seven sections direct the manager to terminate and "immediately request that Flock remove their hardware," bar city employees from searching the city's Flock data, keep existing data "in accordance with applicable law," bar linking it to facial recognition, order a ten-item policy review with "an independent civil-liberties policy reviewer," and freeze new Flock agreements during the review. Axon is not mentioned. ([resolution](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uhGDUZtpecu4I31Y2ePjYNk1AcM4BPXVP7TbSrRbs40/edit) · [agenda](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1KQx06L9DUN1ReoOSCPcS_fwl8cJ1YlypJJjihwGwFWc/edit) · [[Getting Flock Out|the scorecard]])

**August 25, 2026: Council terminates the Flock contract, 5 to 2.** [[Kim Roney|Roney]] moves, [[Sage Turner|Turner]] seconds; [[Antanette Mosley|Mosley]] and [[Sheneika Smith|Smith]] vote no. More than two dozen residents speak, nearly all for termination and nearly all asking council to go further. On the dais, [[Esther Manheimer|the mayor]] confirms the city's existing Axon contract already covers stationary plate readers, none installed, and City Manager [[D.K. Wesley]] gives a verbal assurance none go up "until that community conversation happens." Minutes after passage, Roney's moratorium on purchasing new plate readers during the city's own review **fails for lack of a second**. Full write-up: [[2026-08-25 City Council]].

**August 2026, the region: removal campaigns in four counties.** Henderson County's removal campaign passes 1,000 petition signatures and [[Lowell Griffin|Sheriff Griffin]] publishes a written defense with checkable claims; Brevard schedules a Sept 1 public session on Axon's offer of three free fixed plate readers; Jackson County commissioners hear the sheriff's numbers (1.19 million reads a month from four cameras) while the Sylva Herald's records requests go unanswered; and App State, after finding its cameras were searched 1.5 million times in three months, mostly from out of state, cuts national network access while Boone PD cuts sharing to 41 agencies. County pages: [[WNC/Henderson County/index|Henderson County]] · [[WNC/Transylvania County/index|Transylvania County]] · [[WNC/Jackson County/index|Jackson County]] · [[WNC/Watauga County/index|Watauga County]].
