## Surveillance record

No prior office, so no votes. Two campaign statements, both posted to his campaign's Instagram account:

- **May 14, 2026**, two days after council authorized the RTIC grant and the Axon contract ([[2026-05-12 City Council|the May 12 record]]): "I've been hearing from a lot of my neighbors and constituents that they do not support the RTIC or the Axon agreement. I hear you loud and clear. If I had been on council, I would have voted no. Proud of everyone who showed up and stuck it out on Tuesday night. Your voice matters and will forever live in the public record. Keep organizing, speaking out, and asking questions." ([the reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYVPiZiv3e8/), caption)
- **Aug 25, 2026**, the day council voted to end the Flock contract ([[2026-08-25 City Council|the Aug 25 record]]): "Asheville, I've been listening to you and my stance has not changed since May. To speak plainly: I am against mass surveillance. I support ending our city contract with Flock cameras. I support protecting civil liberties and privacy. See you at 5:00 PM at City Hall." ([the reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DceULhqPx3_/), caption) Whether he spoke at the meeting's public comment is not recorded on this site.
- **Sept 16, 2026**, at a West Asheville candidate forum, asked about surveillance and the Flock vote: "If CPAC or Citizens Police Advisory Committee had been enacted, and it was paused in 2020, would this have been something that came through the pipeline that would have enabled our community to be able to lead and have conversations about this?" (4:13). "There are community members who want this technology and there are community members who don't. And the reason we're at this pivotal time is because we didn't do the work ahead of time" (4:47). "Could I vote on supporting this tomorrow? We haven't done the work" (5:24). (audience recording of the Sept 16, 2026 council candidate forum, held by this site; timestamps are minutes into it.)

He was the first of the six council candidates to state a position on the RTIC or the Flock contract; Drew Ball and Keith Young followed at the Sept 16 forum. Neither statement addresses the questions still open after the Aug 25 vote: a surveillance-oversight ordinance, release of the signed Axon contract and the RTIC's audits, or a council vote before the fixed Axon plate readers already authorized under the contract are installed ([[Getting Flock Out]] · [[Promise Tracker]]).

## The question for him

The River Arts District and downtown business communities are where Fusus's private-camera integration recruits ([[What Is the RTIC]]): business owners will be asked to wire their cameras into the police network. Where does he stand on that? And on the three commitments the record still leaves open: an oversight ordinance, releasing the Axon contract and audits, and a vote before any fixed reader goes in ([[Election 2026#Put the question to them|the questions to ask]]).
