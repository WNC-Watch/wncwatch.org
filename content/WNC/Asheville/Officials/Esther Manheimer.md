## Record on cameras and surveillance

- **January 24, 2023.** Voted for the interlocal agreement joining the [[WNC/Buncombe County/index|Buncombe County]] Sheriff's Fusus camera network; passed 4 to 1 ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-police-get-access-to-buncombe-county-sheriffs-office-fusus-real-time-intelligence-center-camera-system-collaboration-emergency-situations)).
- **March 25, 2025.** Voted for the first [[What Is the RTIC|RTIC]] grant after it was pulled from the consent agenda; passed 6 to 1. [[2025-03-25 City Council|The meeting record]].
- **April 14, 2026.** After more than 40 residents came to speak and the $1.14 million grant item was pulled from the agenda, said the issue "warrants a full conversation before moving ahead" ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/asheville-city-council-punts-vote-on-police-tech-funding-and-suggests-property-tax-hike)). The session that followed was the [[2026-04-28 Work Session|April 28 committee work session]] with Axon and Flock representatives presenting; it took no public comment.
- **May 12, 2026.** Presided over the meeting that approved the RTIC grant and the 7.5-year [[What Axon Contracts Do|Axon contract]], 6 to 1, and voted yes. The item was reached late in the evening; 21 signed-up speakers were not reached. [[2026-05-12 City Council|The meeting record]].
- **May 28, 2026.** At the first mayoral debate, on the cost question: the city already pays the sheriff's office for Fusus access and "we received a grant from the federal government to be able to pay for our own." On the transparency criticism: "That's not a transparency issue. That's a functioning issue." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance))
- **August 19, 2026.** Issued a statement calling for a vote at the Aug 25 meeting to authorize the city manager to terminate the Flock contract: "I have heard clearly from Asheville residents, and I'm calling on Asheville to turn off the Flock cameras and remove them, which requires terminating the Flock contract." On a moratorium instead: "our contract does not allow for the removal of the technology unless we terminate the contract." She also called for a transparent policy on handling and deleting the data collected, and said [[Jackie Stepp|Interim Chief Stepp]] had "already begun this work." ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-mayor-esther-manheimer-vote-end-city-flock-safety-contract-remove-cameras-backlash-automatic-license-plate-readers-data-surveillance) · [828newsnow](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-asheville-mayor-calls-for-vote-to-terminate-flock-camera-contract/))
- **August 20, 2026.** Presenting the resolution at the [[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing|agenda briefing]], disclosed that Flock now requires ten cameras for system access, said removal requires termination because "the cameras are technically not ours," and, asked by [[Kim Roney|Roney]] about a staff memo on "expanding and integrating Axon's automated license plate reader capabilities," confirmed Axon stationary readers are "in the pipeline" while saying "the question around constitutional protection would come back into play." To BPR the next day: "there are some legitimate concerns around Flock to the degree that I think it's time that we terminate the contract and regroup" ([BPR/WHQR, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-08-21/asheville-considers-early-termination-of-flock-but-leaves-door-open-for-other-license-plate-readers)).
- **August 25, 2026.** Voted for the termination resolution, which [[2026-08-25 City Council|passed 5 to 2]]. Answering [[Maggie Ullman|Ullman]]'s question on whether the city had signed for Axon plate readers: "we did, that is one of the things that we voted for. But we haven't put them up... We do not have stationary [Axon] license plate readers yet." On the two vendors: Flock "has an integrated national system where the data does not appear to be secured in a way that any one community can have assurance that their data is not being shared," while "Axon has what's called a closed system or what I'm calling a closed system... We've never had stationary Axon LPRs before, so that's something new that we need to look at." When Roney moved a moratorium on new plate reader purchases minutes after the vote, she did not second it: "I've got enough faith in the manager to do what we just talked about."

## The check-in meetings

Manheimer attends all three of the small-group [[The Check-In Meetings|check-in sessions]] held before each council meeting, each sized at two members plus the mayor, with no notice and no minutes. When Asheville Watchdog reported the practice on January 17, 2023, she said: "If these check-ins are a barrier to folks having confidence that they're getting all the information that the Council is receiving before it makes a decision, then I think we need to move away from that process" ([Watchdog, via Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/from-asheville-watchdog-city-council-mayor-and-staff-hold-closed-door-meetings-sowing-distrust/)). On February 14, 2023, council voted unanimously to replace check-ins with public work sessions ([Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/city-council-check-ins-are-out/)). In 2026 the city acknowledged holding budget check-ins with no agendas ([WHQR/Mountain Xpress, June 30, 2026](https://www.whqr.org/local/2026-06-30/local-government-officials-are-discussing-public-business-behind-closed-doors-is-that-legal)); she describes the practice as "permissible" per legal counsel and has said every member besides [[Kim Roney|Roney]] supports it.
