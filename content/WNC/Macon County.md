## The vote: how it happened

Motion by **John Shearl**, seconded by **Barry Breeden** (a former sheriff's deputy who came in "50-50"), with Chair **Josh Young**, who had leaned toward his sheriff until he asked around town and found the residents who knew about the cameras "almost entirely against them" (the Smoky Mountain News's paraphrase). Shearl, to the Flock rep sent to defend the product: "I don't mean any disrespect, but you are like a car salesman" ([NC Local, July 30, 2026](https://nclocal.org/2026/07/30/balancing-public-safety-and-privacy-wnc-wrestles-with-surveillance-issues/)).

The numbers that did it: **16 cameras (12 county, 4 schools), a $31,950 contract grown to ~$47,000/year, logging 1–3 million plates a month in a county of 37,000.**

## What the meeting video holds that the coverage did not

The full July 14 recording is public ([the meeting video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbuX-GHKoW8), 4 hours 2 minutes). The press covered the vote and the room well. Three things in the recording did not make the coverage:

- **Where the money sat.** At the vote, staff put the contract money at $47,000, sitting in the investigations contracted-services line, and asked what the sheriff should do with it if the motion passed; the discussion that followed turned to moving it to the overtime line, with a suggestion of hiring another deputy ([3:38:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbuX-GHKoW8&t=13085s)).
- **A dispute about how it got there.** Commissioner John Shearl told the sheriff that the former county manager signed the Flock contract in November and that its budget coding was changed from capital to contract services without information provided to commissioners; the sheriff answered that it had not been a secret, and Shearl answered that the first he knew of it was February ([2:39:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbuX-GHKoW8&t=9561s)). The classification matters because a capital request comes before the board and a contract-services payment does not. The paper that would settle the dispute is a records request: the November contract, the manager's signature authority, and the coding change. The same budget mechanics run the other way in [[Henderson County]], where the adopted ordinance lets the sheriff execute contracts inside his approved total without a board vote.
- **An offer nobody has taken up.** The Flock representative sent to defend the contract told the board the company would provide documentation that the county's data had been purged, if asked ([2:47:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xbuX-GHKoW8&t=10046s)). No WNC agency appears to have asked for it. He also told the board the data purges at 30 days, which was the company's stated policy when he said it; in mid-August, Flock announced a reduction from 30 days to 7, so the July number is no longer the company's position.

## What's happened since the vote

- **The four school cameras are still unresolved**: commissioners deferred them to school leaders; the school system says **neither MCSO nor Flock has contacted them** about the cameras on their buildings. No school-board vote has ever been taken.
- **A cautionary note:** the fight's social-media spillover included **false claims that Franklin had Flock cameras** (it has none), escalating to threats against town officials, which handed officials a warning-about-the-opposition story. Getting facts wrong hands officials a weapon; see [[How Cities Won]] on what backfires.

## After the vote: the paperwork that still applies

Removing the cameras did not remove the county from the network. "Macon County NC SO" remains on [[Henderson County]]'s published list of agencies granted access to Henderson's plate reads (Aug 22, 2026); whether anyone in Franklin still logs in is a records question. Macon has no 287(g) agreement with ICE ([ICE list, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/about/offices/ero/287g/participatingAgencies08212026.xlsx)), and its sheriff, like every sheriff in the state, now operates under HB 318's mandatory ICE notification rules ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]). The November commission fields are on [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Macon_County,_North_Carolina,_elections,_2026); a week after the vote, neighboring [[Haywood County]]'s chairman cited Macon by name as the example his county would not follow.
