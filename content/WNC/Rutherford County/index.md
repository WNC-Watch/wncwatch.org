## The damaged camera

The department's July 2026 Facebook post about the damaged camera drew more than 24,000 comments, most of them approving. That volume on a 4,000-person town's page reflects national attention and is not a measure of local opinion.

## The inventory

- **Rutherfordton PD** (Chief Clint Ingle): active Flock cameras (~$3,500 each to replace); count unpublished.
- **Forest City PD**: **14 Flock cameras, including audio detection, plus a private-camera registry.** The audio capability matters legally: North Carolina's wiretap statute (G.S. 15A-287) makes intercepting a conversation one is not party to a felony. Whether audio detection on public streets falls under it has not been addressed by county or town officials on the record as of Sept 8, 2026. ([[Before You Connect Your Cameras]] on private-camera integration)
- **Sheriff's Office**: see the timeline: the February 2025 pitch died without a vote, and the county-owned count remains a records-request target.

## The paperwork

Rutherford has no 287(g) agreement with ICE ([ICE list, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/about/offices/ero/287g/participatingAgencies08212026.xlsx)) and no published sheriff's policy manual. The Sheriff's Office, Rutherfordton PD, and Forest City PD all appear on [[WNC/Henderson County/index|Henderson County]]'s list of 2,048 agencies granted access to Henderson's plate reads (Aug 22, 2026), which is the "nationwide database" the February 2025 pitch was selling. Federal forfeiture payments to the Sheriff's Office: $9,816 (FY2017), $16,834 (FY2020), $53,944 (FY2021), $76,021 (FY2022) ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/afp/fy2024-equitable-sharing-payment-cash-and-sales-proceeds-recipient-agency-state)). What the commission can do about a sheriff's cameras: [[What the Law Lets the County Do]].
