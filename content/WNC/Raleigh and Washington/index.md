Outside the state: Sen. Josh Hawley of Missouri opened a Senate inquiry into Flock on Aug 26, 2026 and extended it to [[Axon]], Motorola Solutions and Verkada on Sept 2, with documents due Sept 16 ([the release](https://www.hawley.senate.gov/hawley-expands-investigation-into-surveillance-cameras-warns-flock-competitors/)). Axon holds Asheville's contract. On Sept 23 his subcommittee held a hearing on Flock's network; the chief executives of Flock, Axon, Motorola Solutions and Verkada were invited and none appeared, and researcher [[Benn Jordan]] and an Arizona sheriff were among the four witnesses ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/23/flock-cameras-draw-bipartisan-concerns-at-senate-hearing/) · [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2026/09/flock-camera-systems-receive-bipartisan-scrutiny-senators/416178/)). On Sept 30 he announced a bill, the Stop Flock Abuse Act: deletion of driver data after ten days outside active investigations, a logged written approval for every search, no sale or sharing with private parties, no facial recognition on plate-reader networks ([the release](https://www.hawley.senate.gov/hawley-to-introduce-stop-flock-abuse-act-establishing-safeguards-for-ai-powered-cameras/)). Rep. Greg Steube of Florida introduced the FLAFO Act on Sept 24, requiring a warrant for federal agencies to use networked plate-reader data ([the release](https://steube.house.gov/press-releases/rep-steube-introduces-flafo-act-to-rein-in-warrantless-flock-safety-surveillance/)). Neither bill has had a vote as of Oct 2, 2026.
