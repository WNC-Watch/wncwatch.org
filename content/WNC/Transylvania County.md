Brevard is WNC's active battleground, a textbook case of [[What Axon Contracts Do|the Axon sales playbook]] running in real time, while the county sheriff's cameras have been up since 2024 with no vote found.

## Timeline

- **August 2024: the sheriff installs first.** Sheriff Chuck Owenby began installing **8 Flock cameras** (no commission vote found), placed on private property near county lines. ([Transylvania Times](https://www.transylvaniatimes.com/news/license-plate-readers-now-watching-highway-traffic/article_cb1ba808-5be4-11ef-8520-ab798f7d6f7b.html) · [[Getting Flock Out]])
- **March 2026: Brevard's original proposal.** Police Chief Christy Wentzell proposed **6 Flock cameras at $46,000 over 3 years**.
- **July 2026: the proposal transforms into an Axon bundle.** Now it's **Axon offering 3 LPR cameras "at no charge,"** inside an **~$80,000 five-year package** with taser and bodycam upgrades, plus monthly data-storage fees. ([Transylvania Times](https://www.transylvaniatimes.com/news/public-input-sought-before-lpr-decision/article_c8ec9c34-eafd-49aa-9777-0f1590911bdd.html) · [[What Axon Contracts Do]])
- **November 3, 2026: Sheriff Owenby runs unopposed.** No challenger filed ([Transylvania Times](https://www.transylvaniatimes.com/news/sheriff-owenby-faces-no-official-opposition/article_dd5fd18d-4e58-4778-bcf9-825f72380725.html)). Two commission seats are open (Jake Dalton and Chase McKelvey are not running).
- **Pending, no date set: the public input meeting.** The Public Safety Committee, with council members Dean Lytle ("hot button") and Aaron Baker pushing, recommended public input before any council vote. That meeting is the single best intervention point in the region.

## The live decision: Brevard

The bundle's fine print is the story: the "free" cameras come with monthly data-storage fees, the part Reading, PA's finance director warned "is where they really make their money." The city attorney reviewed it favorably; the deputy chief previously ran Hendersonville's Flock system.

The opening is real: a small city, a genuinely undecided council, and a decision not yet made. Everything on this site (the [[What Axon Contracts Do|contract record]], [[The Abuse Record]], [[How Cities Won|the Macon playbook]]) is ammunition for whoever shows up.

## The county

Sheriff Owenby credits his 8 cameras with locating six missing persons and "reduction of crime": the usual causal claim, with the usual absence of any independent evaluation. County total per DeFlock: ~13. No transparency portal exists for the sheriff's system, and no policy manual is published; the office does appear on [[Henderson County]]'s list of 2,048 agencies with access to Henderson's reads. Brevard PD publishes three documents on [PowerDMS](https://public.powerdms.com/BrevardPD/documents) (mission, use of force, an organization chart) and no plate-reader policy, consistent with no system yet adopted. Transylvania has no 287(g) agreement with ICE ([ICE list, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/about/offices/ero/287g/participatingAgencies08212026.xlsx)). The sheriff is unopposed in November, which removes the one direct check on his cameras for four more years ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]).

**Video:** county commissioners are on **Vimeo** (no auto-captions); [Brevard City Council is on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCQLjYNExYc71BKYghN0V5iA). The Transylvania Times owns this beat. No organized local opposition group exists yet, which, given the pending public-input meeting, is the gap most worth filling in all of WNC.
