Brevard is the one community in WNC deciding before signing: the draft policy and the [[What Axon Contracts Do|Axon contract]] were posted, the city's question session was held Sept 1, and the vote comes Sept 8. The county sheriff's cameras have been up since 2024 with no commission vote found, and the county board first took the subject up on Aug 24, after residents raised it.

## The live decision: Brevard

The bundle's fine print is the story: the "free" cameras come with monthly data-storage fees, the part Reading, PA's finance director warned "is where they really make their money." The city attorney reviewed it favorably; the deputy chief previously ran Hendersonville's Flock system.

The opening is real: a small city, a genuinely undecided council, and a decision not yet made. Everything on this site (the [[What Axon Contracts Do|contract record]], [[The Abuse Record]], [[How Cities Won|how other communities negotiated]]) is there for anyone who shows up with questions.

## The county

Sheriff Owenby credits his cameras with locating six missing persons and "reduction of crime": the usual causal claim, with the usual absence of any independent evaluation. The count is unsettled: the Transylvania Times reported 8 cameras at installation in 2024, DeFlock maps about 13 countywide, and on Aug 24, 2026 a county commissioner put the sheriff's number at about 14. A briefing from the sheriff, if the board schedules one, would settle it. No transparency portal exists for the sheriff's system, and no policy manual is published; the office does appear on [[Henderson County]]'s list of 2,048 agencies with access to Henderson's reads.

**The county's own meeting record, checked.** We machine-searched the captions of 24 county commission meetings from June 2025 through August 2026 (one, April 13, 2026, has no captions and is not yet covered). Before August 2026 the cameras come up in substance once. At the March 23, 2026 meeting, an annual law enforcement report to the board described the county's speed trailer as carrying a mobile Flock camera, and described the network's reach in the presenter's own terms: once an agency is in the Flock system it can search nationwide, because it is paying into the system ([the county's meeting video](https://www.transylvaniacounty.org/meetings), March 23, 0:19:45). No meeting in the set shows the board considering or authorizing the cameras.

On August 24, after residents spoke at public comment, Vice Chair Larry Chapman asked the board to have the sheriff explain how his office uses the cameras, cited a recent recovery of two abducted girls, and corrected a figure circulating on social media: not 1,400 cameras in the county, about 14 (Aug 24 video, 3:09:48). A second commissioner said he did not know how the cameras had arrived and that the discussion had never entered that forum (3:16:40). The board's next meeting falls after September 11, so the sheriff's briefing, if scheduled, comes after Brevard votes. A resident at the same meeting made a point our own August 27 check of Flock's portals confirms: Henderson County's sheriff publishes a transparency portal and Transylvania's does not, so a Transylvania resident cannot see which agencies have access to the county's reads (0:11:31). The county's video is published on Vimeo without speaker labels, so the lines above are descriptions with timestamps rather than quotations; the caption text is machine-generated and is not reproduced. Brevard PD publishes three documents on [PowerDMS](https://public.powerdms.com/BrevardPD/documents) (mission, use of force, an organization chart); its draft plate-reader policy is posted separately on the city's ALPR page and is read in full below. Transylvania has no 287(g) agreement with ICE ([ICE list, Aug 21, 2026](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/about/offices/ero/287g/participatingAgencies08212026.xlsx)). The sheriff is unopposed in November, which removes the one direct check on his cameras for four more years ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]).

**Video:** county commissioners are on **Vimeo** (no auto-captions); [Brevard City Council is on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCQLjYNExYc71BKYghN0V5iA). The Transylvania Times owns this beat. No organized local opposition group exists yet, which, given the pending public-input meeting, is the gap most worth filling in all of WNC.

## The September decision, dated

- **Tuesday, Sept 1, Mary C. Jenkins Community Center**: the city's ALPR information session, held, staffed by Brevard PD and city staff in a question-and-answer format. It was a city event rather than a noticed meeting, so there is no agenda packet and no minutes, and we have found no recording or press account as of Sept 4. **The draft ALPR policy and the Axon contract remain posted** on [the city's ALPR page](https://www.cityofbrevard.com/1546/ALPR-Program), which by late August also carried the city's own list of questions and answers about the proposal.
- **The decision is a first adoption, not a renewal.** At the Aug 17 council meeting a council member said the city has no plate readers today, that the cameras in the county are run by the sheriff and by a private business, and that concerns about existing cameras belong with the county ([the meeting video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIlTWVz-c6Y&t=7985s), 2:13:05). One week later the county board heard exactly that from residents (above).
- **Tuesday, Sept 8, 5:30 PM, City Council, 95 W Main St**: the decision meeting, ALPR on the agenda. Documents due to the U.S. Senate from Flock's chief executive fall on the same day ([[Garrett Langley]]); the Senate letter concerns Flock, and Brevard's offer is Axon's, so it is context for the week rather than for the vote.
- What is on offer: **three Axon Outpost fixed plate readers at no charge**, inside a five-year, ~$80,000 Axon package of tasers and body cameras, plus monthly data-storage fees. The free camera is the entry ticket; the contract is the product. [[What Axon Contracts Do|Asheville's contract]] shows what the same vendor's terms look like at full size.
- The committee record so far: Council member Aaron Baker wants the city "rock solid on the protections part of it" and says the Axon end of the agreement is his main concern; Dean Lytle says "the chief deserves to answer those questions to the public before it's implemented." The mayor and the other three members have not spoken publicly, and no Transylvania County commissioner has said anything about the sheriff's existing Flock cameras.

## The posted documents, read (Aug 26, 2026)

Brevard posted two documents before deciding, and that sequencing deserves to be said plainly: documents first, questions second, vote last. Asheville signed first and read later. We read both Brevard documents in full; archived copies with checksums are in our records. ([The city's ALPR page](https://www.cityofbrevard.com/1546/ALPR-Program) · [our archive of the page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260827012030/https://www.cityofbrevard.com/1546/ALPR-Program) · [of the contract](https://web.archive.org/web/20260827011632/https://www.cityofbrevard.com/DocumentCenter/View/6444/Axon-Contract))

**The draft policy (General Order 400-32, watermarked DRAFT, signed by Chief Christy Wentzell)** is stronger than most in this region, and the specifics deserve credit:

- Plate data purged at **30 days** by department policy (§D.4); state law, G.S. 20-183.32, allows 90. Asheville's portal showed 90 until the week of its vote.
- **Every query requires a call-for-service or incident report number**, plus the officer's name, date, and reason (§B.3, §F.2). This is materially stronger than a free-text reason box: EFF found 14% of 11.4 million Flock searches nationally justified with the single word "investigation."
- **Two-step approval** before any plate goes on the department's watch list: a supervisor checks for a "documented nexus," then the CID Sergeant confirms (§B.5–7), with 24-hour validation.
- **An annual public report** to the city's Public Safety Committee by Feb 28, "made available to the public," whose required metrics include the number of audits completed and **the number of policy violations discovered** (§H.3).

What the draft leaves open, each phrased as a question the Sept 1 session can answer:

1. **Everything above lives in a General Order**: a document the chief signs and the chief can amend, with no council vote or public notice required (the form's own header carries "Amends" and "Rescinds" fields). That is not a criticism of this chief; it is how General Orders work in any town. The question: will council adopt the retention, sharing, and audit rules in a document only council can change?
2. The policy is **silent on network participation**: whether Outpost data joins any cross-agency lookup, and what the default is on day one.
3. The policy is **silent on Axon's default data program** (see the contract, below).
4. Quarterly audits (§F.1) are internal, run "at the direction of the chief or designee," with no named seat and no publication. Will the quarterly summaries be published, and who, by job title, runs them?
5. **Camera locations** are set by the chief on "data-driven criteria" (§B.2), with no publication requirement. Boone publishes its locations; will Brevard?
6. Sharing (§G) permits disclosure to "a criminal justice officer at a Federal, State, or local law enforcement agency" on written request, with paperwork requirements but **no use-based limits**. Washington State's law names prohibited uses (immigration and reproductive-health enforcement); the documented cases behind those limits are on [[The Abuse Record]].
7. Alert verification is "**should** not rely solely on the ALPR alert" (§B.4), not "shall." The LAPD Inspector General found a 32.3% false-positive rate on stolen-vehicle alerts; a mandatory-verification word change is free.

**The posted contract is Axon's standard Master Services and Purchasing Agreement (version 23, March 2025), all 50 pages of boilerplate.** The actual purchase quote, with Brevard's prices, line items, and subscription term, is not in the posted file; "Outpost" appears nowhere in it. The master terms that matter for a five-year decision:

- **§2.2**: the subscription "will automatically renew for an additional 5-year term," with price increases up to 3% per year in renewal. A five-year deal becomes ten unless someone calendars the exit. That is version 23 of Axon's master terms, dated March 2025. The one signed Axon contract posted in full in North Carolina, Durham's from June 2026, is version 25, in which the automatic renewal is gone, renewal is by mutual written agreement, and some price caps became Axon's then-current list price ([[What Axon Contracts Do]]). Which version Brevard would sign, and what sets the price at renewal in that version, is not answered by anything the city has posted.
- **§3 and §6**: "Payment obligations are non-cancelable." "All sales are final."
- **§17**: termination only for uncured breach or non-appropriation of funds; and if hardware was sold below sticker price, an early exit is billed at "the difference between the MSRP... and amounts paid" (§17.3). The free camera is an advance, recovered at sticker price if the city leaves.
- **Cloud appendix §3**, credit where due: "Customer controls and owns all rights, title, and interest in Customer Content," with US data stored in-country.
- **Cloud appendix §13**: for usage data, "including query logs and metadata," Axon is an "independent Data Controller," processing it for its own product purposes, with third-party disclosure permitted once "anonymized, de-identified, and/or aggregated."
- **The ACEIP appendix**: "By default, Customer will be a participant in ACEIP Tier 1," Axon's program for using customer content across customers to develop its products, with privacy-preserving extraction. Opting out requires the customer to revoke consent in writing. The draft policy does not mention it.

For comparison, Denver asked before signing and negotiated a one-year term, a camera cap, no national-database participation, and a per-query audit trail ([[The National Wave]]). Every one of those protections was winnable by a customer that asked first, which is exactly the position Brevard is in until Sept 8.
