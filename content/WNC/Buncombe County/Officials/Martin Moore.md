**Title:** Buncombe County Commissioner, District 2 & commission vice chair · Democratic nominee for District Attorney · attorney, former public defender · on track to be Buncombe's first Black DA ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-03-06/martin-moore-to-be-buncombe-countys-first-black-district-attorney))
**In this role since:** December 2022 (elected Nov 2022 with 63%)
**Current term ends:** —
**Next voter decision:** **Nov 3, 2026**: DA general election; won the March 3 Democratic primary (39.29%, over Booth's 36.03% and Kurdys's 24.7%) and no Republican filed, so the nomination is effectively decisive; would take office as DA January 2027 · [[Election 2026]]

## Why the DA matters here

The outgoing DA, Todd Williams, **wrote a letter supporting the [[What Is the RTIC|RTIC]]** that APD read into the record on [[2026-05-12 City Council|May 12]]. The DA's office is where surveillance becomes prosecution: whether plate-reader hits become stops, whether RTIC-derived evidence gets scrutinized or rubber-stamped, and how discovery obligations around camera networks are honored. The 4th Circuit's pending Norfolk ruling ([[Communities That Ended or Limited Contracts|Communities that ended or limited camera contracts]]) could land on his desk as binding law.

## Surveillance record

His first public statement on surveillance technology found is his October 2026 response to Asheville DSA's candidate pledge, below; none was found before it (searched through Oct 7, 2026). Two votes are on record: on April 2, 2024 he seconded the board's 5 to 0 approval of $250,000 in forfeiture money "to purchase surveillance equipment" ([minutes](https://media.buncombenc.gov/common/commissioners/minutes/2024-04-02.pdf)); on Oct 6, 2026 he seconded [[Drew Ball]]'s motion removing the sheriff's $290,000 FUSUS/Axon forfeiture request from the agenda ([county video, 0:02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1XbypN6jVfM&t=173s)). Neither carried a stated reason in the meeting record; his October statement gives one for the second, below. His public priorities center on gun and domestic violence. His public-defender background is the most interesting variable: he has seen surveillance evidence from the defense side.

- **Oct 2026.** Declined to sign the candidate pledge on surveillance sent by Asheville Democratic Socialists of America (Asheville DSA), a local political organization, whose commitments include a judicial warrant before surveillance of a private individual and an end to contracts with Flock Safety and Axon (Fusus) ([[Election 2026#Asheville DSA's pledge on surveillance, October 2026|the responses]]). From his statement: "I am declining to sign the Asheville DSA pledge, and I want to be candid about why." On evidence: "as a general rule, I believe it is detrimental to the integrity of the role to make a pre-determined choice not to consider types of evidence that our courts deem lawful in our state and country, whether or not we personally object to them." On data sharing, as a commissioner: "data from local law enforcement cameras should be shared with outside agencies only under clear policies and in line with the law, including a warrant where the law requires one." On the Oct 6 removal he seconded: "My goal was to receive more information about the request and confirm safeguards are in place."
