Asheville is this collection's home case, documented across forty-plus pages. This page is the regional view: the same timeline-first format as its [[WNC and NC|WNC neighbors]], so you can lay the city's buildout beside Hendersonville's, [[Macon County|Macon]]'s, or Woodfin's and see the shared playbook: deploy quietly, brief late, vote last. Each entry links to the page that documents it in full.

## Timeline

- **December 2022: Fusus access begins, no vote.** APD signs an MOU with the [[Buncombe County]] Sheriff's Office for access to the county's Fusus platform: "We've had limited access since 2022," [[Jackie Stepp|Interim Chief Stepp]] later confirmed ([[2026-04-28 Work Session|April 28, 2026 session, 1:01:29]]). The platform was in police hands before any elected body voted on it.
- **January 24, 2023: Council buys in, 4–1.** The interlocal agreement: APD access to the county's ~1,800-camera Fusus network, $30,000/year. Housing Authority director David Nash speaks in favor; the ~500 cameras in housing communities come with no resident consent process ([[Watching the Poor]]). **[[Kim Roney]] casts the lone no**, a position she will hold alone for three years. ([Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/asheville-joins-buncombe-county-surveillance-system/))
- **April 2024: Mobile plate readers deployed.** LPRs go live on APD's fleet vehicles. Council is not briefed; there is no vote. ([[2025-03-07 Agenda Briefing|admitted a year later]])
- **2024: The earmark nobody voted for.** APD and the office of U.S. Rep. [[Chuck Edwards]] arrange a federal RTIC earmark; accounts differ on who initiated it. Edwards signs the request May 22, 2024. Council is not informed. ([[Chuck Edwards]] · [[Timeline]])
- **September 24, 2024: One informational session, three days before Helene.** A ~$60K JAG grant "to start the outfit of a real-time intelligence center" gets an informational committee session (no vote), then the storm buries the thread. ([[2025-03-25 City Council]])
- **February 2025: The Flock contract.** APD contracts for 11 stationary Flock cameras. ([[2026-04-28 Work Session|Stepp's own timeline, 1:03:04]])
- **March 7, 2025: Council finds out.** First-ever briefing, held only because Roney read about the cameras in the news: mobile LPRs running ~11 months, stationary cameras in the community "approximately two years," access to "**over 177,000 cameras across the Eastern US**"; "and we've had that access." Full write-up: [[2025-03-07 Agenda Briefing]].
- **March 25, 2025: First RTIC money, on consent.** $71,999 slid between bulletproof vests and DEA grants; Roney's 29 questions unanswered, her delay request refused; [[Bo Hess|Hess]]: "we're not buying surveillance, we're buying chairs" while conceding the center "already exists." Passes 6–1. Full write-up: [[2025-03-25 City Council]].
- **July 31, 2025: The committee hears a different number.** APD tells the [[The Committee|Public Safety Committee]] the LPR network is "connected to over 19,000 cameras" (March's figure: 177,000) and that immigration alerts exist but are "turned off." The committee never requests an audit, then or ever. ([[Oversight Theater]] · [[Their Claims vs The Record]])
- **February 2026: The $1.14M lands.** The Edwards earmark arrives, DOJ-administered, conditions published only after acceptance. ([[Timeline]])
- **April 14, 2026: Pulled from consent.** After public pressure, the RTIC item is pulled "to create some awareness"; residents deliver an hour of documented opposition. ([[The People Who Showed Up]])
- **April 28, 2026: The sales pitch.** Axon and Flock reps flown in, no public comment; Durham's "peer city" example collapses live; Roney reads the Flock contract's "irrevocable, worldwide license" clause into the record. Full write-up: [[2026-04-28 Work Session]].
- **May 7, 2026: "That does not exist yet."** Five days before the vote, the city manager confirms the Axon contract council will authorize hasn't been written; the "keep one Flock camera" plan is explained as a network-access subscription. Full write-up: [[2026-05-07 Agenda Briefing]].
- **May 12, 2026: The midnight vote.** After six hours, 22 of 23 speakers opposed, 21 more sent home unheard, the motion seconded before public comment began: **6–1**, Roney alone. $1.14M accepted, 7.5-year Axon authorization granted. Full write-up: [[2026-05-12 City Council]].
- **August 2026: The reassurance tour.** APD's explainer interview: 11 cameras, 18 authorized searchers, "you have to put the reason" (the safeguard a U.S. Senate investigation had already found meaningless). ([[What They Can Actually Learn About You]] · [828NewsNow](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-flock-cameras-asheville-police-explain-use-of-11-license-plate-readers/))
- **August 29–September 1, 2026: The G20 arrives.** Finance ministers, federal security, road closures, with Asheville's cameras inside the same national mesh agencies have provably searched for "protest." ([[Who Gets Watched]])
- **~February 2027: Flock contract runs out.** The "one camera" decision point: contract-lapse or renewal. The four-part exit motion is written and waiting: [[Getting Flock Out]].
- **Spring/summer 2027: RTIC fully operational (projected).** The oversight promises come due long after public attention has moved on. ([[Promise Tracker]])
- **2033: Axon contract ends.** Or doesn't: renewal terms are the vendor's, unless council is made to demand better. ([[What Axon Contracts Do]])

## The pattern, for regional comparison

Read against the county pages, Asheville's sequence is the playbook at maximum scale: infrastructure first (2022–2024), briefing only when caught (March 2025), money on consent (March 2025), committee bypass (2026), vendor-led "education" (April 2026), vote on an unwritten contract at midnight (May 2026). Macon's commissioners reversed the same playbook with one 5–0 vote ([[How Cities Won]]); Hendersonville and Woodfin are mid-story. Asheville followed the same playbook at larger scale, but until the RTIC goes operational in 2027, it can still be stopped or constrained.
