Western North Carolina is not passively accepting this technology. One county has already removed it. Several towns have canceled. The organizing wins are close enough to drive to.

## Macon County took its cameras down

On July 14, 2026, Macon County commissioners voted **5–0** to end their $47,000 Flock contract. Twelve cameras came down immediately. ([Smoky Mountain News](https://smokymountainnews.com/news/news-latest/de-flocking-macon-county-votes-to-let-alpr-contract-lapse/) · [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/macon-county-north-carolina-wnc-flock-remove-license-plate-reader-cameras-unanimous-vote-privacy-concerns-public-safety-debate-ai-artificial-intelligence-surveillance))

The numbers that moved them: the sheriff's office was logging **1 to 3 million plates a month in a county of about 37,000 people**. On the record:

- Commissioner John Shearl, invoking the Fourth Amendment: "I don't want a hunch. I want the truth."
- Chairman Josh Young: "the people that I represent... they don't want the cameras."
- Commissioner Barry Breeden: "there's a lot of unknowns in where the data goes."
- A resident at public comment: "I love my government, but I don't trust you as much as I trust my mother."

And a fact Asheville's council should notice: **the opposition was led substantially by Republicans.** The Macon County GOP organized against Flock; Republican commission candidate Dylan Castle campaigned against it at public comment. When the most successful anti-surveillance politics in the region is coming from conservative counties, this isn't a left-right fight. It's residents versus the agencies watching them, and residents are winning where they organize.

## Where the data goes: one county's list

Flock's transparency portals let each agency publish the organizations it shares data with. [[Henderson County]]'s sheriff has: **2,048 agencies in more than 40 states**, including the FBI, the US Postal Inspection Service, an Air Force base, a HIDTA task force, five state police forces, and 212 North Carolina agencies from the SBI down to UNC Asheville's campus police. Every WNC sheriff's office on this page except Clay's neighbor Cherokee appears on that list as a recipient, including the ones (Madison, Haywood, McDowell, Transylvania) that publish no portal of their own. By contrast App State's campus police share with five agencies and Boone with six. The difference is a setting, chosen by a sheriff, with no law requiring it to be disclosed. The statute that allows it: [[What the Law Lets the County Do]].

## Who has signed with ICE

ICE's participating-agencies list (August 21, 2026) shows 29 North Carolina 287(g) agreements. In the west: **Henderson County Sheriff's Office** (Jail Enforcement Model, June 9, 2020), **Cherokee County Sheriff's Office** (Warrant Service Officer, March 10, 2025), **Avery** and **Caldwell** county sheriffs (Warrant Service Officer, 2020), and **Newland PD** (Task Force Model, October 17, 2025). Buncombe, Haywood, Madison, Jackson, Macon, Transylvania, McDowell, Rutherford, Polk, and Watauga have none. The SBI, the Highway Patrol, and the Department of Public Safety, which a 2026 state law orders to sign, had not as of the list date ([ICE](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/about/offices/ero/287g/participatingAgencies08212026.xlsx)). Since October 2025 every county jail in the state must notify ICE before releasing anyone charged with a felony or impaired driving, agreement or not; the laws, with text: [[What the Law Lets the County Do]].

## Elsewhere in NC: the cancellations

- **Hillsborough**: canceled October 2025 (~$81.5K, 10 cameras) over data-privacy concerns.
- **Pittsboro**: rejected Flock in 2023, terminated again May 2026, both 4–1.
- **Chatham County**: cameras removed April 2026.
- **Live fights right now:** Wilmington/New Hanover County, Southern Pines/Moore County, Davidson County, Garner (~$449K proposal), and **Apex**, where residents drafted a full [ALPR governance ordinance](https://deflockapex.org/docs/apex-alpr-governance-ordinance.pdf), the closest thing NC has to a model local law. ([DeFlockNC](https://www.deflocknc.org) · [deflockapex.org](https://deflockapex.org))

## The state is going the other way

While counties remove cameras, Raleigh is installing them. The **July 2026 state budget made the SBI's highway plate-reader program permanent**: about 140 highway camera locations across 17 agencies and growing, with the SBI's stated ambition of "a camera at every entrance and exit to the state." ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/nccapitol/surveillance-drivers-camera-alpr-license-plate-reader-nc-budget-public-safety-privacy-july-2026/) · [Daily Tar Heel](https://dailytarheel.com/article/city-ai-highway-surveillance-north-carolina-budget-20260728) · [UNC School of Government analysis](https://nccriminallaw.sog.unc.edu/license-plate-readers-highway-pilot-program-and-new-offense/))

**What NC law actually requires** ([G.S. 20-183.30 et seq.](https://www.ncleg.gov/Laws/GeneralStatuteSections/Chapter20)): a written policy, a 90-day retention cap, confidentiality, and a Class 1 misdemeanor for unauthorized access. **What it doesn't do:** unlike Illinois, California, or Washington, North Carolina law says *nothing* about sharing your data with federal agencies or across state lines. That silence is exactly what Asheville's 500-mile sharing radius drives through, and it means the protections Asheville residents get are only as strong as APD's own revisable policies. See [[Promise Tracker]]. The statute's sharing clause, the sheriff-commissioner divide, the forfeiture money, and the three immigration laws passed over vetoes since 2024 are walked through on [[What the Law Lets the County Do]].

## NC's first criminal charges for camera misuse (July–August 2026)

- **July 29, 2026**: a Charlotte-Mecklenburg officer became the **first in NC criminally charged** for an unauthorized Flock search.
- **August 5, 2026**: a Mooresville officer was arrested for stalking his ex-wife through Flock: **31 searches**. Eleven more officers reportedly under investigation.
- Statewide precedent for the charge: unauthorized ALPR access is a misdemeanor under the same statute APD operates under. The mechanism that caught these officers (audit logs) is the mechanism [[Get Involved|CAMS]] is demanding be made public in Asheville. ([NCLocal](https://nclocal.org/2026/07/30/balancing-public-safety-and-privacy-wnc-wrestles-with-surveillance-issues/))

When someone says "this is just what cities do now," the answer is a list: Macon, Hillsborough, Pittsboro, Chatham, Boone.
