## Surveillance record

On February 3, 2025 Ellenburg pitched commissioners an 8-camera Flock network (6 fixed, 2 portable); his detective sergeant's selling point was the network itself: "If you get the 8, you have access to the nationwide database." Commissioners asked about cost, bandwidth, and DOT permits and took no vote, then or since ([meeting summary](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/7297288/north-carolina/rutherford-county/North-Carolina/Rutherford-County/Sheriff-urges-board-to-consider-Flock-license-plate-camera-network-presentation-prompts-questions-on-cost-privacy-and-DOT-permits) · [Annie Dance](https://anniedance.substack.com/p/exclusive-details-rutherford-county-commissioners-meeting-feb-3-2025)). Later campaign coverage refers to "11 license plate reader cameras" and a vehicle "caught at the border"; the date and source of that figure are unconfirmed and the county-owned count remains a records question ([[Rutherford County]]). His own framing of the office: "We've got seven officers covering 566 square miles on a good day" ([Annie Dance](https://anniedance.substack.com/p/exclusive-sheriff-ellenburg-launches-reelection-bid)).

The Sheriff's Office appears on [[Henderson County]]'s list of agencies with access to Henderson's reads, so the "nationwide database" is available to his deputies whether or not the county ever bought a camera.
