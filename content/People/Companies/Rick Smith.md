**Title:** Founder and Chief Executive Officer, Axon Enterprise (TASER International at founding, 1993) · Scottsdale, Arizona
**Next voter decision:** none; he answers to shareholders

## Record

Smith built the taser company into the public-safety bundle Asheville bought: body cameras, in-car cameras, drones, the Fusus real-time platform (acquired 2024), and, since 2025, fixed plate readers that mount inside streetlights ([[Axon]]). Under his leadership Axon proposed taser-armed drones for schools in 2022 over its own ethics board's unanimous objection; nine board members resigned and the plan was paused ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon_Enterprise)). In December 2025 the company announced it was resuming facial-recognition research on body cameras, "responsibly and transparently," with a Canadian police pilot ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/newsroom/announcements/exploring-the-future-of-responsible-facial-recognition)). His framing, from the company newsroom: "We see ourselves as a technology company committed to making the world a safer, less dangerous place." Revenue under him reached $904 million in the second quarter of 2026 alone, up 35% ([Axon blog, on its Freethink interview](https://www.axon.com/blog/axons-vision-for-responsible-innovation-in-public-safety)).

## Why he is on this site

Every promise Asheville officials made about the RTIC is a promise about what Axon's platform will and will not do. The facial-recognition ban in Policy 1703 binds APD staff; it does not bind a vendor that has announced it is evaluating the feature ([[The RTIC Policy]] · [[Promise Tracker]]). The city's contract term outlasts the current mayor, chief, and city manager; it will not outlast him.

## The question for him

Will Axon publish, for every agency, which other agencies can search its fixed-reader data, and will it contractually bar facial recognition on feeds from cities that have banned it?
