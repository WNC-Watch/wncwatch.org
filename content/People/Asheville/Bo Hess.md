**Title:** City Council member · **chairs the Public Safety Committee** · behavioral-health provider/social worker; council's only Latino member
**In this role since:** Dec 2, 2024 (elected Nov 2024, first term)
**Current term ends:** Dec 2028
**Next voter decision:** not on a ballot until Nov 2028 · [[Election 2026]]

## Surveillance record

The [[What Is the RTIC|RTIC]]'s most vocal advocate on council. Voted yes on [[2026-05-12 City Council|May 12, 2026]], and authored the accompanying "civil liberties resolution" stating the technology would be used "solely for lawful purposes, which are intended to enhance or provide for public safety", a statement of intent with no enforcement mechanism, no reporting duty, and no penalties. ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-13/asheville-council-accepts-police-tech-funding-hears-budget-proposal))

## In his words

At the [[2025-03-25 City Council|March 25, 2025 council meeting]], arguing for the first RTIC funding ([BPR, Mar 26, 2025](https://www.bpr.org/bpr-news/2025-03-26/last-night-at-council-asheville-approves-funding-for-police-surveillance-hub)):

> "When you actually responsibly deploy technology like drones and real time intelligence, what you do is you actually de-escalate the risk for officers and our citizens." And: "You're removing the need to send an officer blindly into a dangerous situation and that lowers the chance of use-of-force incidents." He pointed to Durham: "They didn't throw out public safety, they modernized it."

He also claimed, same meeting [(37:05)](https://www.youtube.com/live/Bc-VVlvBx0U&t=2225s): "Over 95% of our APD officers are trained in crisis intervention"; the last public figure was 90%, and DOJ's evidence clearinghouse rates CIT "Ineffective" at reducing use of force.

**The full researched counter (no study anywhere has ever measured drones or RTCCs reducing use of force; the false-positive record runs the other way; and his own example city, Durham, rejected ShotSpotter, declined an RTCC, and scaled *unarmed* response instead) is in [[Their Claims vs The Record|Their Claims vs. The Record]].**

Three more of his on-record claims, from the transcript archive:
- **The "chairs" defense** (Council, March 25, 2025, [32:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bc-VVlvBx0U)): "Just to be clear, the real time intelligence center **already exists**. We're not buying surveillance. **We're buying chairs** and workstations for the officers to do their job." He repeated it minutes later: the center "already exists... The funding buys basic infrastructure like desk, chairs, workstations." The defense contains an admission: the surveillance center *already existed*, built and operating before council ever voted on it. And the reassurance didn't even hold within the meeting: Chief Lamb, answering questions moments later, said APD didn't yet have "the Fusus software... or the finalized agreement with Axon."
- **The bias claim** (Public Safety Committee, July 31, 2025): license plate readers "would actually eliminate a lot of the bias from officers... they wouldn't be pulling over like profiling." The record cuts against it: LAPD's audit found a [[The Abuse Record|32.3% false-positive rate]] that produces guns-drawn stops of innocent people, and those stops don't fall evenly. Automated stops don't remove bias: the false hits fall unevenly too, and the machine makes the stop look neutral.
- **The one-camera argument** ([[2026-05-07 Agenda Briefing|Agenda briefing, May 7, 2026]]): keeping "that one Flock camera is a strategic public safety move," and removing all of them "puts every single 100,000 residents at risk"; "until Axon has the type of database that we can access like Flock." His own condition concedes the point: the plan preserves the national search account until the vendor is swapped, not until the surveillance ends. See [[Getting Flock Out]].
- **The emergency-only claim** ([[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing|Agenda briefing, Aug 20, 2026, 1:06:45]]): defending the RTIC as the Flock termination vote approached: "the RTIC and Flock are two different things. RTIC is a real-time, emergency-activated system... **This is not general surveillance**... only activated when there is an emergency or someone calls 911," closing: "I don't think we should come up here and spin and lie, especially in a time when everyone is so anxious." Beside it, [[The RTIC Policy|the RTIC's own written policy]], released that same morning: RTIC staff "may monitor relevant cameras" **"based on crime trends** or active incidents" and are assigned to "public events, large crowds, and special operations." The emergency-only description is not what the rulebook says, and the rulebook was written by the department he's charged with overseeing.

Two more threads from the committee record, in his own words:

- **His conversion story** (council, May 7, 2026): "I had to sit with public safety officials, IT folks, tour the place. I had to like hear things over and over before I really started to understand it." By his own account, he was convinced by repeated sessions with the people selling and operating the system, not by independent research. The [[The Flockbook|leaked vendor playbook]] calls this the pre-brief; he's describing it from the inside.
- **The panhandling defense** ([[The Committee|Public Safety Committee]], July 31, 2025): after eleven callers opposed expanding the solicitation ordinance, he explained that opponents misunderstand "the way that science and data collection works," insisted "in no way are we criminalizing homelessness or poverty," and invoked his own practice: "this is my job. I work in this every day... These are my people." The committee advanced the expansion 3–0 that morning. By November, APD reported the output to the same committee: **eight operations, 93 people charged.** Whatever the intent, 93 people charged is the result. In the same November meeting, his tribute to the retiring chief cited "an over 36% reduction in violent crime"; the actual annual figure, reported that January, was 24% ([[Their Claims vs The Record]]).

**On keeping Axon, Aug 21, 2026.** Previewing the Flock vote, Hess opposed termination and backed APD's planned shift to Axon plate readers: body cameras are "what keeps everybody safe," and Axon "holds... officers accountable" ([BPR/WHQR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-08-21/asheville-considers-early-termination-of-flock-but-leaves-door-open-for-other-license-plate-readers)). The vendor swap in one sentence ([[Getting Flock Out]]).

**On ICE, November 2025.** When immigration operations were expected in Asheville he said, "I stand firmly for our constitutional rights, for the safety of every resident and for the dignity of every person who calls Asheville home," relayed that "We received that information from Governor Josh Stein. We were told that several vans would be brought to Asheville," and told residents "You are not alone, and you belong here" ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-ice-border-patrol-immigration-enforcement-resolution-buncombe-county-north-carolina-governor-josh-stein-city-council-bo-hess-kim-roney-community)). Six months later he voted for a system whose sharing clause permits disclosure "as permitted by law" to any agency, and chairs the committee that has never asked what that means ([[The RTIC Policy]] · [[Who Gets Watched]]).

## Zero oversight from the committee he chairs

Hess chairs the Public Safety Committee, the body where RTIC oversight is supposed to live. A review of all seven of its recorded meetings (May 2025–July 2026) found **zero**: no audits requested, no post-approval briefings received, and the **July 29, 2026 meeting, held after the RTIC was operational-bound, contains not one word about RTIC implementation, the Flock wind-down, or the promised quarterly audits.** The only skeptical questions ever raised in that committee came from [[Antanette Mosley|Mosley]] (on ICE agreements) and [[Sheneika Smith|Smith]] (on profiling and face-vs-plate), never from its chair. His committee next meets **Sept 24, 11 AM**. See [[Oversight Theater]].

## Why he matters now

His committee, Public Safety, meets the last Thursday of odd months at 11 AM, virtually ([livestream](https://publicinput.com/d81053)), and is where RTIC implementation reporting will land. If the quarterly audits (see [[Promise Tracker]], row 4) surface anywhere public, it's there. He is also the natural council author for any move to convert his resolution's language into an actual ordinance, a test of whether the civil-liberties framing was substance or cover. Residents who want that on the record should ask him directly: bohess@avlcouncil.com. See [[Speak at Council]].
