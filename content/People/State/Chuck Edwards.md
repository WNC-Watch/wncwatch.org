**Title:** U.S. Representative, NC-11 (R), the congressman who secured the [[What Is the RTIC|RTIC]]'s $1.14 million federal earmark
**In this role since:** January 2023 (previously NC state senator)
**Current term ends:** January 2027; he withdrew from the 2026 race on Aug 5, 2026, after the House Ethics Committee recommended his censure ([[Jennifer Balkcom]] replaced him on the ballot Aug 10)
**Next voter decision:** He isn't on the **Nov 3, 2026** ballot (that race is [[Jennifer Balkcom]] vs [[Jamie Ager]]), but he holds the office, and the accountability, through January 2027 · [[Election 2026]]

He is on his way out. He is also the reason the money exists. Council's May 12 vote was framed as a choice about accepting funding: funding that existed because Edwards requested it, twice, in writing. Those letters are public, and they are the weakest part of the whole paper trail.

## The paper trail: two signed letters, one sentence of justification

Members of Congress must sign and publish a disclosure letter for every Community Project Funding request. Edwards signed one for the RTIC on **May 22, 2024** ([his office's PDF](https://edwards.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/edwards.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/APD%20RTIC%20Financial%20Disclosure_RepEdwards.pdf) · [local copy](../assets/edwards-rtic-fy25-letter-2024-05-22.pdf)) and a nearly identical one on **April 28, 2025** ([PDF](https://edwards.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/edwards.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/apd-rtic-financial-disclosure_repedwards-1%29.pdf) · [local copy](../assets/edwards-rtic-fy26-letter-2025-04-28.pdf)). The entire written case for the project, in both letters, is this:

> "This project is a good use of federal funds because it will improve operational efficiency and enhance community safety, which would enable the Asheville Police Department to protect and serve residents more effectively."

That is the full justification. No data, no crime figures, no cost analysis, no comparison to alternatives: one sentence, submitted twice, a year apart, word for word. Set it beside what the sourced record actually shows about these systems: [[Their Claims vs The Record]] · [[The Abuse Record]].

**The request doubled with no explanation.** The FY2025 request was **$529,692** ([his FY25 disclosure page](https://edwards.house.gov/fy25-community-project-funding)). The FY2026 request was **$1,141,255.50** ([FY26 page](https://edwards.house.gov/services/fy26-community-project-funding-requests)), more than double, with zero change in the justification text. No public explanation for the increase has been found. His **FY2027 request list contains no RTIC item.**

**The timing.** Per [CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/), the funding was requested in 2024 **without Asheville City Council being informed**: the May 22, 2024 letter predates council's first RTIC vote conversation by months, and predates the [[2026-05-12 City Council|May 12, 2026 vote]] by two years. By the time council "decided," the money was already sitting there. See [[Timeline]].

**Who called whom: an open question.** CAMS and [BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/asheville-city-council-punts-vote-on-police-tech-funding-and-suggests-property-tax-hike) report the earmark came at APD's request. Mountain Xpress, citing a city report, describes it the other way: **Edwards' office contacted APD in April 2024** about potential funding, and the department pursued it. Both versions trace to city documents; the underlying report would settle which door opened first. That's a records request; see [[Records Center]].

## What he said when he was selling it

His one known on-record interview advocating for the project: WLOS, June 2024, under the headline ["NC Congressman Edwards pushes for $500k AI initiative to fight Asheville crime"](https://wlos.com/news/local/nc-congressman-chuck-edwards-pushes-500000-ai-initiative-fight-crime-rtic-real-time-intelligence-center-artificial-intelligence-asheville-police-department):

> "It's easy to surmise that we have a crime problem in Asheville... We definitely have a real problem out there, and it's my intent to not only criticize the problem but to help where I can... I recognize [this] by helping to bring a half a million dollars to Western North Carolina to these police officers to do their job, and it's simply the right thing to do."

Two things about that interview hold up badly:

1. **The crime claim already had an on-record rebuttal, from the sheriff who built the camera network.** At Edwards' own 2023 anti-crime summit, Sheriff [[Quentin Miller]] said Edwards' crime characterization was "not supported by statistics" and called it "irresponsible to have a conversation not rooted in data"; former sheriff Van Duncan added that Buncombe's crime rate was down 9% year over year ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/anti-crime-summit-in-asheville-sparks-debate-on-safety-underlying-issues-in-western-north-carolina-us-congressman-chuck-edwards-buncombe-county-sheriff-quentin-miller-former-sheriff-van-duncan-asheville-police-chief-david-zack-mayor-esther-manheimer)). When APD later made its own shifting crime-number claims to sell the same project, the same problem appeared: [[Their Claims vs The Record]].
2. **It was sold as an "AI initiative."** That's WLOS's framing of the project in June 2024, in a story built on Edwards' cooperation. Two years later, selling the same center to a skeptical public, APD emphasized what the system would *not* do: no facial recognition, no AI analysis ([[Promise Tracker]]). The two descriptions contradict each other.

When the FY26 money passed, his office claimed credit in a [January 12, 2026 release](https://edwards.house.gov/media/press-releases/edwards-votes-advance-fy26-cjs-energy-and-water-and-interior-funding-packages): "$1 million for the Asheville Police Department to establish a Real-Time Intelligence Center," under his quote about "ensuring federal dollars are spent responsibly and directed where they make the greatest difference at home."

## What he said when Asheville pushed back: nothing found

Through the April 14 meeting where [[The People Who Showed Up|40+ residents]] forced the vote off the agenda, the May 12 midnight vote, the "Shame!" chants, and the months of coverage since, **no statement from Edwards or his office about the RTIC has been found**: not in BPR, Mountain Xpress, WLOS, or 828NewsNow coverage, and not in his own releases (his January 2026 appropriations recap lists five NC-11 projects and omits the RTIC). Absence of a found statement isn't proof he said nothing anywhere, but the pattern is: advocacy in 2024 when it was a crime-fighting announcement, silence in 2026 when it became a fight.

## The 2020 record: Asheville's police budget as a target

This isn't his first time routing around Asheville's elected government on policing. In 2020, as a state senator, after city council shifted $770,000 out of APD's budget, Edwards called the decision "an absolute travesty" and "bowing to the radicals that are asking for police departments to be defunded," and said the state "has the responsibility to insist a city takes actions", while developing legislation to strip state funds from the city ([Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/edwards-discusses-retaliation-against-asheville-for-police-funding-shifts/)). In 2020 he moved to punish Asheville's council for a police-budget decision; in 2024 he routed federal police-tech money past that same council entirely. The consistent through-line is that Asheville's elected officials weren't the ones deciding.

## What's still his to answer

He holds the office through January 2027. Questions that are still live: Why did the request double? What analysis, if any, sat behind the one-sentence justification? Who initiated the April 2024 contact? He also remains a named figure in the [[Records Center]] requests that would answer them.
