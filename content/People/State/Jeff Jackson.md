**Title:** Attorney General of North Carolina · Democrat (former U.S. Representative, NC-14, and state senator)
**In this role since:** January 1, 2025
**Current term ends:** January 2029

## Record on these issues

Despite a targeted search of the Department of Justice's releases and press coverage, **no statement, letter, guidance, or enforcement action by Jackson on ALPR, Flock, data sharing, or ICE cooperation was found** (as of Aug 22, 2026). The first criminal charges in the state for plate-reader misuse, against a Charlotte-Mecklenburg officer (July 29, 2026) and a Mooresville officer (August 5, 2026), were brought on SBI affidavits, not by his office ([WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/politics/2026-07-30/cmpd-investigates-officer-after-sbi-computer-access-charge-alleging-flock-system-misuse) · [WBTV](https://www.wbtv.com/2026/08/05/mooresville-police-officer-accused-using-flock-cameras-track-boyfriends-ex-wife/) · [[The Abuse Record]]).

**A correction for the record:** an earlier research note on this site flagged a possible "Jackson letter" read at Asheville's May 12, 2026 vote. The letters read that night were from District Attorney [[Todd Williams]] and U.S. Attorney Russ Ferguson ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-13/asheville-council-accepts-police-tech-funding-hears-budget-proposal) · [[2026-05-12 City Council]]). No letter from the Attorney General was involved.

## The question for him

The statute his office would enforce permits plate data to reach federal agencies on a written request and requires no log. Does the Attorney General have a view on whether that is adequate, and would his office issue guidance to the 100 sheriffs who operate under it? ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]])
