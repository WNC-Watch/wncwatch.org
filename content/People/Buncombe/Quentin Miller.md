**Title:** Buncombe County Sheriff · Democrat
**In this role since:** December 2018 (elected Nov 2018; re-elected 2022)
**Current term ends:** December 2026
**Next voter decision:** **Nov 3, 2026**: seeking a 3rd term vs [[Gary Parris]] (R); had no primary challenger · [[Election 2026]]

## Surveillance record

The architect of mass surveillance in [[Buncombe County]]. His office built the region's Fusus [[What Is the RTIC|real-time intelligence center]] around 2022: **~1,800–2,000 cameras, including roughly 1,500 inside county schools**, plus 20 stationary and 2 mobile plate readers, with 59 people authorized to run Flock searches. Asheville paid $30K/year for access starting 2023; the city's RTIC is this network's expansion. ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/2-000-camera-network-feeding-090126108.html) · [Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/local-government/wtf-buncombe-sheriffs-office-surveillance-technology-explained/))

He publicly credits the network for county crime reductions ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/crime-takes-a-dive-in-buncombe-county-sheriff-credits-community-connection-innovative-technology-north-carolina-state-bureau-of-investigations-annual-crime-report-real-time-intelligence-center)), claims the state's own SBI data undermines: his "15% countywide drop" was actually his unincorporated-county slice; **countywide violent crime rose 12.7% the same year** (a point first made publicly by [[Chuck Edwards]], who later funded Asheville's RTIC); by 2024 his jurisdiction's crime was **rising against a falling state baseline**, with burglary +42%; and camera-less Catawba County outperformed Buncombe by 17 points over the same window. **No independent evaluation of his RTIC has ever been conducted.** Full data workup with SBI tables: [[Their Claims vs The Record]]. His office touts a Fusus advisory board ("the only sheriff's office in the state" with one), though its membership, powers, and minutes are not public.

## His rulebook, in his own words

His office publishes its policies, and they are more candid than his public statements ([[Buncombe County|full breakdown]]). The RTIC policy he approved on April 9, 2025, three years after the center opened, describes "proactive monitoring" and using cameras near festivals and concerts "to look for suspicious activity leading up to and during the event," the language APD later cut from its own draft. His plate-reader policy (March 2023) allows sharing with any "criminal justice agency" for "active criminal intelligence operations," with no mention of federal agencies, and assigns the annual audit to his Fusus coordinator "along with sworn members of the FUSUS Advisory Committee." In 2022 he described that body to the Citizen Times as a community advisory board, "the only sheriff's office in the state that has an advisory board." In the policy, its auditors are deputies. No membership or audit has ever been published. His drone policy was revised August 15, 2026 to add a "Drone as a First Responder" program. And the "not tax dollars" funding he cites is federal forfeiture money: the Department of Justice paid his office about $988,000 in equitable sharing across eight reported years, with the largest payments during the RTIC build-out. ([BCSO Policy Manual](https://public.powerdms.com/buncombe/tree/documents/2407055))

**His center, by its own log.** The Fusus audit log his office released in May 2026 covers 28 months: 58 percent of camera viewing on public housing, 30 percent on schools, almost all of it live, half of it by three accounts, 309 plate searches in total, and every timestamp withheld. His own account viewed cameras 34 times ([[Sheriffs Audit Log|The Sheriff's Audit Log]]).

## In the policy workgroup's notes (June 2026)

Miller sat on the city's private [[The RTIC Policy|RTIC policy workgroup]], and APD's own meeting notes preserve his framing candidly: "the RTIC is **not an invasion of privacy**, but a tool used only when requested"; audits "serve **internal purposes rather than the public directly**, our messaging must emphasize that this technology enables real-time oversight of officer activity"; and on money, "**seized drug money** initially funded this expensive system" at his office, grants are "temporary relief," and long-term sustainability "requires the community to embrace the program." He also made the workgroup's best suggestion, recorded and not adopted: "consider having **those who are opposed** join us at the table in these discussions."

## The other side of his record: limiting ICE

The record should hold this honestly: Miller has taken documented stands **limiting federal reach**: questioning ICE detainers on constitutional grounds and barring ICE from county schools without court orders. ([Bolts](https://boltsmag.org/buncombe-county-district-attorney-sheriff-elections-and-state-preemption/)) The tension is structural: the network he built is exactly the kind of infrastructure that outlives any one sheriff's restraint. A successor inherits the cameras; the ICE commitments are his alone.

**The federal side contests his record.** In 2025 Sen. [[Thom Tillis]] labeled Buncombe a "sanctuary jurisdiction"; Miller called it "blatantly incorrect," saying "There is not a single BSCO policy that would in any way limit our cooperation with federal law enforcement or immigration services" and citing 15 detainers processed under HB 10; DHS dropped the county from its list in August 2025 ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/bpr-news/2025-06-09/buncombe-co-labeled-immigrant-sanctuary-is-blatantly-incorrect-sheriff-miller-says) · [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/buncombe-county-department-homeland-security-sanctuary-jurisdiction-list-north-carolina-sheriff-qunetin-miller-senator-thom-tillis-house-bill-10-hb10-immigration-customs-enforcement-ice)). In February 2026 DHS publicly disputed a detainer case, quoting his office's November 2025 statement that "We cannot and will not use county resources to subsidize the enforcement of civil immigration violations"; the office replied, "Without fail, the Buncombe County Sheriff's Office follows the law and will continue to do so," and confirmed it is not in the 287(g) program ([WLOS, Feb 11, 2026](https://wlos.com/news/local/buncombe-county-sheriffs-office-department-homeland-security-immigration-customs-enforcement-north-carolina-asheville-ecourts-ice-nationality-act-western-north-carolina-house-bill-318-section-287-law-citizenship)). Both things are true at once: he resists ICE at the jail door within what the law allows, and his plate-reader policy lets the data go to any criminal justice agency for "intelligence operations" ([[What the Law Lets the County Do]]).
