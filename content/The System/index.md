This section explains what was bought and what it does: what the RTIC is, what the cameras collect, what the data becomes once it joins the network, and what the county's own records show about how the system is actually used. The pattern that runs through every page: the camera is the visible part; the decisions that matter are the network settings, the sharing lists, and the audit rules, most of which were never brought to a public vote.

- **[[What Is the RTIC]]**: Plain-language explainer on what was actually approved: the center, the cameras, the sharing radius, the retention, the vendors.
- **[[What They Can Actually Learn About You]]**: What a plate scan becomes: your routes, your associations, your name, from court records, audit logs, and APD's own briefings.
- **[[Who Gets Watched]]**: Where a network lookup actually reaches: immigrant families, people traveling here for reproductive care, and protesters, documented from audits and court filings.
- **[[Watching the Poor|Cameras in Public Housing]]**: About 500 cameras already watch Asheville's housing communities. What the record shows depends on who governs them, and for whom.
- **[[Sheriffs Audit Log|The Sheriff's Audit Log]]**: Twenty-eight months of the county's own Fusus access records: 58 percent of camera viewing on public housing, almost all of it live, most of it by three accounts, every timestamp withheld.
- **[[Follow the Money]]**: The BID, the TDA, the earmark, and the forfeiture funds: who pays for the buildout and who asked for it.
- **[[Before You Plug In|Before You Connect Your Cameras]]**: For business owners: what integrating your cameras with the police network actually signs you up for, and the standalone alternative that keeps you useful to real investigations.
